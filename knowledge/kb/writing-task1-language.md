## 2. Describing the size and speed of a change: adjectives and adverbs

## 3. Two sentence patterns for every trend

Board notes (line graph lesson):

- **Verb pattern:** *The number of books **rose to** 90000 in Italy in 2000.*
- **Noun pattern:** *An increase / an upward trend **was observed in** the number of books in Germany in 2000, **standing at** 120000.*
- **Fluctuation:** *The number of books **fluctuated between** 70000 and 90000 in Italy in the first two years.*
- **Stability:** *The number of books **remained stable at** 90000 in Italy in the first two years.*

More noun-pattern frames from the models:
- *There was a sudden / sharp / steady + noun + in + subject.* (*there was a sudden increase*)
- *Subject + saw / experienced / witnessed + adj + noun.* (*this figure saw a decline to about 35 in 2014*, *the dolphin population experienced substantial growth*)
- *Subject + exhibited a steady upward trend, starting at … and reaching …*

Prepositions with numbers:
- **rose to** 90 (final value) · **rose by** 20 (the size of the change) · **from** 9% **to** 17% · **stood at / standing at** 27 · **reaching / culminating in** 55 · **a peak of** 14% · **between** 19 **and** 25 million

## 4. Number, amount, quantity, percentage, proportion

Board notes:

| Word | Use with |
|---|---|
| **The number of …** | countable nouns (c): *the number of tourists* |
| **The amount of …** | uncountable nouns (uc): *the amount of money / water* |
| **The quantity of …** | countable and uncountable (c/uc) |
| **The percentage / proportion / rate of …** | countable and uncountable (c/uc) |

So: *the amount of money* ✓ · *the quantity of money* ✓ · *the number of money* ✗

**Quantities** (synonyms for the subject): *Number / Quantity / Amount* · *Percentage / Proportion / Rate* · *Figure / Indicator*

### "The figure for…" to avoid repetition

**The figure / indicator + for** = a paraphrase of the subject:

> *The amount of money spent on books was 90 in Germany in 2000, while **the figure for** Austria was twice less than that of Germany, standing at 45 in the same year.*

Also: *that of…* (*the number of dolphins surpassed **that of** seals*), *the corresponding figure for males*.

### Verbs + number or percentage

> *The amount of money spent on books **was / made up / constituted / consisted of** 90 in Germany in 2000.*
>
> *The figure for Germany **makes up / constitutes / accounts for** 40%.*

**Other verbs:** to make up · to account for · to comprise · to amount to · to consist of · to constitute · to equal · to be equal to

Verbs + noun / adjective + noun (a way to describe trends): *… made up an increase.* · *… made up a significant rise.* · *Accounted for the majority.*

### Multiples

> *The amount of money spent on books had risen twice to 90 in Germany by 2000.*
> *The amount of money spent on books **had doubled** to 90 in Germany by 2000.*

*A two-fold increase · doubling · a five-fold increase · tripled (the Cancer Centre tripled in size) · halved (which ultimately halved the size of the original car park) · nearly twice as much as …*

## 6. Comparisons

| Structure | Example from the models |
|---|---|
| *X was the highest / lowest …* | *Exports to Japan were the highest at the beginning…* |
| *X + verb + more / less than Y* | *…which was $10,000 less than Italy.* |
| *nearly / almost twice as much as* | *…12.1%, which was nearly twice as much as that of the civil service (5.6%).* |
| *compared with* | *50.3% of males rode bicycles, compared with a slightly higher figure of 51.4% for females.* |
| *whereas the corresponding figure for … was …* | *…whereas the corresponding figure for males was considerably lower, at 24.6%.* |
| *X surpassed / overtook Y* | *By 2012, they had surged to approximately 27 billion, overtaking Japan and the US…* |
| *the second / third highest* | *…becoming the second dominant source.* |
| *joint second-place* | *…the joint second-place holders, France and Switzerland, at around $70,000.* |
| *X outnumbered Y* | *UK residents traveling abroad always outnumbered foreign visitors.* |
| *respectively* | *…comprised 7.8% and 7.3% respectively.* |
| *followed by / This was followed by* | *This was followed by the manufacturing industry at 16.3% and education at 14.7%.* |
| *relative to / among the three* | *…making it the highest initial share relative to the three.* |

## 7. Prepositions of time and time expressions

From the handbook's time-expressions sheet:

| Preposition | Example |
|---|---|
| **in** | In 1999… · In the 20th century… · In the first ten years… *there was a significant increase in production* |
| **for** | For the first six months… · For twenty years… *the number of employees remained the same.* |
| **during** | During the first six months… · During the first half of this century… · During the remainder of the year… *production was slowing down, while imports increased.* |
| **from – to / until** | From August to / until November… · From 1950 to 1960… *there was no change in energy use.* |
| **between – and** | Between 1950 and 1960… *there was no change in the rate of fuel consumption.* |
| **before / after** | Before 1960 *the number remained small,* but after 1965 *there was a sudden increase.* |
| **around / about** | Around / about 1980 *there was a change in the number of female part-time employees.* |
| **by** | By the late 19th century *the rural workforce had declined significantly.* |
| **at** | At the end of the last century *there was a sharp increase in manufacturing.* |
| **since** | Since the 19th century *there has been a steady decline.* |

Other useful expressions: (in) the period from ___ to ___ · (in) the period between ___ and ___ · in the first/last three months of the year · over the period ___ to ___ · over the next years / decades / quarter of a century · over a ten year period · throughout the 19th century · from that time on · after that · then · in the 1980s · at the beginning / by the end of the period · in the final year · over the period shown

### Tense depends on the preposition (board notes)

| | Prepositions | Past data | Future data |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Simple** | in · from…to · between…and · during · throughout · over · at · around | *The amount of money spent on books **rose** to 90 in Germany **in** 2000.* | *The amount of money spent on books **will rise** to 90 in Germany **in** 2030.* |
| **Perfect** | **by** · **until** | *The amount of money spent on books **had risen** to 90 in Germany **by** 2000.* | *The amount of money spent on books **will have risen** to 90 in Germany **by** 2030.* |

> Rule: **by + year** goes with the **perfect** (*had + V3* for the past, *will have + V3* for the future). Model: *By 2012, they had surged to approximately 27 billion.* / *By 1980, notable changes had occurred.*

## 8. Prediction structures (future data)

*"Prediction structures are commonly used to describe trends based on the given data, maps, or graphs. These sentences often appear in conclusions or future-oriented analyses."* Use them when a chart shows **projected** years (e.g. 2030, 2040) or a map shows **planned** changes.

| # | Structure | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subject + will + verb + object + preposition + time** | *The GDP will rise dramatically up to $415 billion in 2023.* · *The number of obesity cases in the city will decrease sharply this year.* |
| 2 | **According to current + plural noun, there will be + subject + preposition + time** | *According to current plans, there will be 2 million refugees living in camps in 2022.* · *According to current predictions, there will be 1000 new positive Covid cases in this city within 2 months.* |
| 3 | **It is + past participle + that + subject + will + verb + object + preposition + time** | *It is planned that the government will sponsor 1000 scholarships for students whose parents died from COVID-19 this year.* · *It is predicted that the eastern mainland of Ho Chi Minh City will be completely submerged by 2050.* |
| 4 | **Subject + is/are + past participle + to + verb (infinitive) + preposition + time** | *By 2023, Vietnam is expected to reach its export target.* |
| 5 | **Subject + is/are + likely + to + verb (infinitive) + preposition + time** | *An aging population is likely to lead to a shortage of workers in technology sectors in 2030.* |

Variations: *is projected / forecast / predicted / expected to + verb* · *is likely / set to + verb* · *it is estimated that…* · *will have + V3 by 2040* (see §7).

**Overview with future data:** mention both the past trend and the projection, without numbers. For example: *Overall, the proportion of the population living in cities increased in all four countries and is expected to keep rising until the end of the period.*

**Tip:** vary the structures. Don't use *will* in every sentence. Use a different prediction structure for each category in the body paragraphs.
