# Advantages / Disadvantages Essay

## How to recognise it

- *What are the advantages and disadvantages (of this / for the child of …)?*
- *Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.*

The question does **not** ask which side is stronger. If it says *"Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?"*, write an [outweigh essay](writing-task2-outweigh.md) instead.

## Paragraph plan (teacher's handout)

| Paragraph | Length | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | 2–3 sentences | **Paraphrase the prompt**: rewrite the background topic using synonyms and altered sentence structures. **Thesis statement**: state clearly what advantages and disadvantages you will discuss, and **choose the same number of advantages and disadvantages**. |
| **2. Body paragraph 1: the advantages** | 5–6 sentences | **Topic sentence**: state the primary benefit or positive impact of the topic. **Explanation 1**: explain why or how this advantage happens. **Example 1**: a concrete, real-world example to support your point. |
| **3. Body paragraph 2: the disadvantages** | 5–6 sentences | **Topic sentence**: introduce the major drawbacks or negative aspects of the topic. **Explanation 1**: detail how this disadvantage affects people or society negatively. **Example 2**: a specific example of this downside in action. |
| **4. Conclusion** | 1–2 sentences | **Summary**: summarise the main points by briefly restating the core advantage and disadvantage you discussed. |

## Sentence frames from the models

| Purpose | Frame |
|---|---|
| Paraphrase | *These days, many parents provide their children with …, ranging from … to …* / *Nowadays, the increasing popularity of … has led to a decline in …* |
| Thesis | *While this practice offers clear advantages, such as …, it also introduces significant drawbacks that warrant careful consideration.* |
| Thesis (alternative) | *While this shift offers clear benefits regarding … and …, it also introduces notable drawbacks concerning … and …, making it a development with equally balanced positive and negative impacts.* |
| Advantage topic sentence | *One prominent merit of … is its ability to …* / *One of the most significant advantages of … is …* |
| Second advantage | *Another key benefit is …, as …* / *Furthermore, …* / *Finally, …* |
| Explanation | *This happens because …* / *Since …, both … and … save …* / *As a result, …* |
| Disadvantage topic sentence | *Conversely, an oversupply of … can severely hinder …* / *However, the widespread adoption of … is not without drawbacks.* |
| Explanation | *When …, they often …* / *This lack of … directly impedes …* / *In addition, …, meaning that …* |
| Example | *For instance, when …, they naturally …* / *For example, a university student can now …* / *A clear illustration of this is seen when …* / *A clear illustration of this occurs when …* |
| Conclusion | *In conclusion, while … can serve as a catalyst for …, it simultaneously …* / *In conclusion, … provide undeniable …; however, these benefits are countered by …* |

Synonyms: **advantage**: *merit, benefit, strength, positive aspect, upside*. **Disadvantage**: *drawback, downside, negative aspect, limitation*.

## Model essay 1

**Question:** *Some parents buy their children a large number of toys to play with. What are the advantages and disadvantages for the child of having a large number of toys?*

> These days, many parents provide their children with a vast array of playthings, ranging from creative tools and puzzles to advanced technological gadgets. While this practice offers clear advantages, such as accelerating cognitive development, it also introduces significant drawbacks that warrant careful consideration.
>
> One prominent merit of providing a diverse selection of toys is its ability to stimulate a child's psychological and intellectual growth. Educational materials, such as building blocks and strategy puzzles, effectively hone creativity and critical problem-solving faculties. Furthermore, physical or interactive toys play a crucial role in refining fine motor skills and hand-eye coordination. Finally, tools that mimic real-world environments, such as medical kits or miniature kitchens, cultivate vital social and emotional intelligence through collaborative role-playing. This happens because building things forces young children to look closely at shapes and follow a clear plan from start to finish. For instance, when a child spends hours successfully assembling a complex Lego castle, they naturally develop advanced spatial awareness, structural engineering concepts, and deep patience.
>
> Conversely, an oversupply of playthings can severely hinder a child’s social integration and creative resourcefulness. When children are given an endless stream of solitary entertainment, they often become insular, preferring to isolate themselves in their rooms rather than engaging in active outdoor play or face-to-face communication with peers. This lack of social exposure directly impedes the development of essential interpersonal skills like empathy, negotiation, and conflict resolution. Furthermore, when toys are highly structured and complete, they do all the mental work for the child, leaving absolutely no room for natural imagination or cognitive resourcefulness. Instead of learning to invent their own games out of simple, everyday objects, children become entirely dependent on pre-manufactured external stimulation to cure their boredom. A clear illustration of this is seen when children obsessively collect viral Pop Mart figures like Labubu, leading to material competition and peer exclusion instead of friendly social interaction.
>
> In conclusion, while an extensive collection of toys can serve as a catalyst for multifaceted learning and skill acquisition, it simultaneously risks children's social development and eroding their innate imaginative capabilities.

*(345 words)*

**Analysis**

- *Advantage:* cognitive and intellectual growth (creativity, problem-solving, motor skills, social-emotional intelligence). The example is assembling a **Lego castle**.
- *Disadvantage:* it hinders social integration and imagination. The example is collecting **Pop Mart / Labubu** figures, which leads to competition and exclusion.
- The conclusion repeats one advantage and one disadvantage without taking a side.

## Model essay 2

**Question:** *With the rise of e-books comes the decline in paper books. Some people see this as a good step forward while others do not. What are the advantages and disadvantages of this?*

> Nowadays, the increasing popularity of e-books has led to a decline in the use of traditional paper books. While this shift offers clear benefits regarding convenience and accessibility, it also introduces notable drawbacks concerning reading habits and technological dependence, making it a development with equally balanced positive and negative impacts.
>
> One of the most significant advantages of e-books is cost-efficiency. Since electronic books do not require printing, binding, or physical shipping, both publishers and consumers save a substantial amount of money. As a result, readers can buy a wide variety of titles at a much lower price than printed versions. Another key benefit is the flexibility that digital readers offer, as individuals can store hundreds of books on a single device and carry them easily while traveling. This convenience is especially helpful for students or professionals who need to access multiple textbooks at the same time. For example, a university student can now carry an entire semester's worth of heavy reading materials inside a single lightweight tablet.
>
> However, the widespread adoption of e-books is not without drawbacks. Many readers argue that digital screens cannot replace the physical pleasure of holding a real book and turning its actual pages. For avid readers, this traditional sensory experience is an essential part of literacy that technology simply cannot replicate. In addition, electronic reading is completely dependent on digital devices, meaning that users are vulnerable to battery loss and unexpected technical glitches. A device can easily run out of power or malfunction, making it unreliable during emergencies. A clear illustration of this occurs when a traveler is left unable to read their novel during a long flight simply because they forgot to bring a charging cable.
>
> In conclusion, e-books provide undeniable cost savings and unmatched portability; however, these benefits are countered by the loss of the physical reading experience and a vulnerable dependence on battery life.

*(312 words)*

**Analysis**

- This one has **two advantages** (cost-efficiency, flexibility/portability) and **two disadvantages** (loss of the physical reading experience, dependence on devices/battery). The numbers are equal, as the teacher's plan requires.
- The examples are scenarios rather than statistics: a university student with one tablet, and a traveller without a charging cable. A realistic scenario is an acceptable example.

## Notes

- **Balance**: the same number of points on each side, and paragraphs of similar length.
- No personal opinion is required. If you add one, keep it short and put it only in the conclusion.
- Use **contrast linkers** to open Body 2: *Conversely, / However, / On the other hand,*
