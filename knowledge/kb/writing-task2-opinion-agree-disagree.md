# Opinion Essay (Agree / Disagree)

## How to recognise it

The instruction asks for **your opinion on one statement**:

- *Do you agree or disagree?*
- *To what extent do you agree or disagree (with this statement / opinion)?*

There is only **one** statement to judge. If the question gives two views ("Some people think… while others believe…"), it is a [discussion essay](writing-task2-discussion.md).

## Paragraph plan (teacher's "OPINION ESSAY - STRUCTURE")

| Paragraph | What to write |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | Paraphrase the question. **Give your opinion.** **State two supporting reasons.** |
| **Main body paragraph 1** | **Topic sentence**: outline the 1st reason for supporting this view. **Explanation**: explain this idea. **Example**: give an example. |
| **Main body paragraph 2** | **Topic sentence**: outline the 2nd reason for supporting this view. **Explanation**: explain this idea. **Example**: give an example. |
| **Conclusion** | Summarise your opinion and the key reasons. |

Both body paragraphs support **your** side. You do not need a paragraph for the opposite view. Model 1 deals with it in a single "While…" clause in the introduction and conclusion.

## Sentence frames from the models

| Purpose | Frame |
|---|---|
| Paraphrase | *In today's world, many people choose to … instead of …* / *… is widely regarded as one of the most critical … on Earth.* |
| Concession + opinion | *While … is definitely important, the value of … should not be ignored.* |
| Thesis (disagree) | *I disagree with the idea that …, because …* |
| Thesis (agree) | *I completely agree with this perspective. This issue leads to … because …, while …* |
| Body 1 opener | *To begin with, … plays a massive role in …* |
| Body 2 opener | *Furthermore, …* / *Similarly, …* |
| Contrast inside a paragraph | *On the other hand, …, regardless of …, because …* |
| Example | *A great example of this is seen in …, where …* / *This situation is clearly shown by business research from companies like …, which consistently proves that …* / *For example, statistics published by … reveal that …* / *For instance, a recent … study by … confirmed that …* |
| Conclusion | *In conclusion, while … offers clear …, I strongly believe that …* / *In conclusion, … is undoubtedly the primary threat to …* |

Words for strength of opinion: *I completely agree / I strongly believe / I firmly disagree / I disagree with the idea that / I completely agree with this perspective.*

## Model essay 1: Disagree

**Question:** *A big salary is much more important than job satisfaction. Do you agree or disagree?*

> In today's world, many people choose to focus on earning a high salary instead of finding a job they truly love. While having enough money is definitely important, the value of enjoying your work should not be ignored. I disagree with the idea that a big paycheck is more important than job satisfaction, because long-term happiness and health depend heavily on how happy you are with your daily work.
>
> To begin with, job satisfaction plays a massive role in a person's mental health and everyday happiness. When people spend more than forty hours a week at a job, the emotional environment of the workplace changes their entire life. Doing work that matters makes people feel successful, proud, and emotionally stable, which naturally protects them from anxiety and depression. On the other hand, a high-stress job that feels meaningless quickly destroys a person's mood, regardless of how much money they make, because human happiness relies on purpose rather than just a large bank account. A great example of this is seen in charity organizations like Doctors Without Borders, where doctors willingly give up the huge salaries of private hospitals to help people in need, leading to deep personal happiness despite working in dangerous areas.
>
> Furthermore, workers who love their jobs are generally much more creative, productive, and loyal to their companies. When an employee feels genuinely excited about their duties, they do not just do the bare minimum; instead, they invest their personal energy into finding better ways to solve problems. This high level of commitment makes them stand out to their managers, which naturally leads to fast career growth, promotions, and eventually, even higher pay over time. Therefore, passion does not mean staying poor; it is actually a reliable path to true financial success because excellent performance is driven by genuine interest. This situation is clearly shown by business research from companies like Gallup, which consistently proves that happy and connected workers make their teams 21% more profitable than teams that are unhappy and disconnected.
>
> In conclusion, while a big salary offers clear material benefits, I strongly believe that loving your career is much more important for long-term happiness and health.

**How it follows the plan**

- *Intro:* paraphrase ("many people choose to focus on earning a high salary instead of finding a job they truly love"), then a concession ("While having enough money is definitely important…"), then the opinion (**I disagree**) and the reasons (long-term happiness and health).
- *Body 1:* Reason 1 is mental health and happiness. The explanation covers forty hours a week and purpose versus a bank account. The example is **Doctors Without Borders**.
- *Body 2:* Reason 2 is that happy workers are more creative, productive and loyal, which leads to promotion and even higher pay. The example is **Gallup** research (21% more profitable).
- *Conclusion:* restates the opinion and the reasons in one sentence.

## Model essay 2: Agree (to what extent)

**Question:** *Overpopulation is the world's most serious environmental problem. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this statement?*

> A surging global population is widely regarded as one of the most critical environmental crises on Earth. I completely agree with this perspective. This issue leads to severe ecological degradation because the expansion of human settlements causes massive deforestation, while the resulting rise in consumer demands drastically worsens atmospheric pollution.
>
> The rapid growth in the number of individuals worldwide creates a severe shortage of available housing. To tackle this housing crisis, human settlements are constantly expanded into natural habitats and forests, leading to the destruction of valuable natural resources. This aggressive expansion directly disrupts delicate ecosystems because clearing massive wooded areas reduces the planet's capacity to absorb carbon dioxide and produce oxygen. This process leaves the environment vulnerable to long-term climate damage if strict conservation measures are not taken in advance. For example, statistics published by National Geographic reveal that millions of acres of forest are cleared every year to make room for residential housing and industrial zones, causing a noticeable reduction in global oxygen levels.
>
> Similarly, population density directly fuels environmental contamination due to the massive reliance on transportation. An increasing number of residents automatically results in to a higher volume of vehicles on the road, not only in crowded urban centers but also in rural areas. As more individuals use private and public transport to commute from one place to another, the quantity of greenhouse gas emissions released into the atmosphere rises dramatically. Furthermore, this intense crowding generates severe noise pollution that disrupts both human health and local wildlife. For instance, a recent atmospheric study by NASA confirmed that the Earth's air quality is deteriorating at an unprecedented rate due to the toxic gas emissions generated by daily human activities.
>
> In conclusion, overpopulation is undoubtedly the primary threat to the natural world. The uncontrolled growth of the human population inevitably leads to the overexploitation of natural habitats and the severe contamination of our atmosphere.

**How it follows the plan**

- *Intro:* paraphrase, then **"I completely agree with this perspective."**, then two reasons (deforestation from expanding settlements, and pollution from consumer demand).
- *Body 1:* the housing shortage leads to expansion into forests and reduces the planet's capacity to absorb CO₂. The example is **National Geographic** statistics.
- *Body 2:* reliance on transport increases greenhouse gas emissions, plus noise pollution. The example is a **NASA** atmospheric study.
- *Conclusion:* "undoubtedly the primary threat", plus the two reasons restated.

> Note: the handout contains a small slip, *"results in to"*. The correct form is **results in**.

## Notes and common mistakes

- **Pick one side and keep it.** The same opinion must appear in the introduction, both topic sentences and the conclusion.
- For *"To what extent…"* you may agree completely (model 2) or partly, but say so clearly in the thesis: *I completely agree / I partly agree*.
- Each body paragraph needs **one** reason, fully developed (explanation, result, a **named** example). Two thin reasons in one paragraph score lower than one well-explained reason.
- Give the two reasons in the thesis in the **same order** as the body paragraphs.

## Practice questions from real exams (shared by the teacher)

- In many countries, there is a rising rate of juvenile delinquency because more young mothers work and therefore spend less time caring for their children. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
- Some people believe that drivers should take a driving test every year. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
- Some people believe that countries should protect their cultural identity by insisting that all new buildings are built in a traditional style. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion?
- Money should be spent on creating new public buildings such as museums or town halls rather than renovating the existing ones. To what extent do you agree or disagree?
- Governments should concentrate more on pollution and housing problems than on helping people to prevent illnesses. Do you agree or disagree?
