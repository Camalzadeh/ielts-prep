# Cause / Solution Essay

The course practised this type the most. It came up in class (crime rates, students' concentration, homelessness) and in the mock exams.

## How to recognise it

Two questions: **why** the problem happens and **what can be done**.

- *What are the causes of this problem? What can be done to deal with this situation?*
- *What are the causes of this problem? What can be done to solve the problem?*
- *What are the causes of crime and what could be done to prevent this rise in criminal activity?*

## Paragraph plan (teacher's "THE IELTS TASK 2: CAUSE/SOLUTION STRUCTURE")

**1. INTRODUCTION**
- **Sentence 1: Paraphrase the topic.** Rewrite the topic statement in your own words using synonyms.
- **Sentence 2: Thesis statement.** Clearly state the main cause and the main solution you will discuss.

**2. BODY PARAGRAPH 1: THE CAUSE**
- **Sentence 1: Topic sentence (introduce the cause).** State the main reason why the problem is happening.
- **Sentences 2 and 3: Explanation.** Explain how or why this cause leads to the problem. Go deeper into detail.
- **Sentence 4: The result.** Show the direct consequence or outcome of this cause on society.
- **Sentence 5: Real-world example.** Provide a short, specific example (mentioning a country or organization) that proves your point.

**3. BODY PARAGRAPH 2: THE SOLUTION**
- **Sentence 1: Topic sentence (introduce the solution).** State the main action that needs to be taken to fix the problem.
- **Sentences 2 and 3: Explanation.** Explain exactly how this solution works and who should implement it (e.g. governments, schools, individuals).
- **Sentence 4: The result.** Show the positive outcome or change that happens once this solution is applied.
- **Sentence 5: Real-world example.** Provide a short, matching example of a country that successfully used this solution and saw a drop in the problem.

**4. CONCLUSION**
- **Sentence 1: Restate the thesis / summary.** Summarise the main cause and the main solution in one final sentence.

## The teacher's fill-in template (built from her two models)

> **Intro:** *An increasing number of / An increasing amount of* [topic, paraphrased]. *This essay will examine how* [cause 1] *and* [cause 2] *cause this problem, and explain why government action is the most realistic solution to this global issue.*
>
> **Body 1:** *The primary reason why* [problem] *is* [cause 1]. *When* [situation], [chain of explanation]. *This means that* [consequence], *leaving them with no choice but to* [result]. *The second major cause is* [cause 2]. [Explanation]. *As a result of* [cause 2], [result]. *For instance, reports show* [example].
>
> **Body 2:** *The primary solution is for governments to* [solution 1]. *When states* [action], *they* [effect]. *This allows* [people] *to* [positive result]. *The second step is for* [actor] *to* [solution 2]. *When* [change], [effect]. *As a consequence of* [change], [result]. *For example,* [country] *recently introduced* [policy]. *Eventually,* [problem] *dropped significantly.*
>
> **Conclusion:** *In conclusion, a combination of* [cause 1] *and* [cause 2] *causes* [problem]. *However, this issue can be solved if governments* [solution].

The students' mock-exam essays used exactly this frame (*"This essay will examine how … cause this … issue and explain why government action is the most realistic solution to that problem."*).

## Model essay 1: Two causes, two solutions

**Question:** *An increasing number of professionals, such as doctors and teachers, are leaving their own poorer countries to work in developed countries. What are the causes of this problem? What can be done to deal with this situation?*

> An increasing number of skilled professionals, such as school teachers and medical doctors, are leaving developing nations to work in richer countries. This essay will examine how unemployment and low salaries cause this problem, and explain why government action is the most realistic solution to this global issue.
>
> The primary reason why these experts choose to migrate is the high level of unemployment in their home countries. When developing nations face economic problems, there are not enough jobs created for university graduates. This means that even qualified individuals cannot find work, leaving them with no choice but to look for jobs abroad. The second major cause is the low salary given to those who find work. In poorer nations, wages are so low that workers cannot afford the basic cost of living. As a result of these low salaries, they feel unhappy and are forced to leave their homes to find a better life elsewhere. For instance, reports show thousands of teachers leave Asia annually due to long periods of unemployment and poor pay.
>
> The primary solution is for governments to create more jobs. When states invest in building schools and hospitals, they open up many positions for graduates. This allows educated people to use their skills at home instead of moving away. The second step is for employers to increase the basic salary. When local wages are raised, professionals can easily cover their daily living costs. As a consequence of these higher salaries, they feel valued and choose to stay in their homes to build a better future locally. For example, the Philippines recently introduced laws to upgrade medical salaries. Eventually, health worker migration dropped significantly, keeping talent inside the country.
>
> In conclusion, a combination of high unemployment and poor wages causes professionals to leave. However, this issue can be solved if governments improve working conditions and give experts a strong reason to stay.

*(315 words)*

**Analysis**

| Step | In the essay |
|---|---|
| Cause 1 | unemployment: not enough jobs for graduates, so they have to look abroad |
| Cause 2 | low salaries: workers can't afford the cost of living, so they are forced to leave |
| Example | thousands of teachers leave Asia every year |
| Solution 1 | governments create jobs (build schools and hospitals) |
| Solution 2 | employers raise salaries, so workers feel valued and stay |
| Matching example | the **Philippines** raised medical salaries and migration dropped significantly |

## Model essay 2: One cause, one solution, developed in depth

**Question:** *There is too much noise in many public places in cities. What are the causes of this problem? What can be done to solve the problem?*

> An increasing amount of noise pollution is severely affecting public spaces in modern cities. This essay will examine how urban overpopulation causes this problem, and explain why government action through better city planning is the most realistic solution to this issue.
>
> The primary reason why public places are becoming too loud is city overpopulation. Urban areas are crowded because thousands of people migrate from rural regions every year in search of better job opportunities and a higher standard of living. When cities face this massive influx, popular central squares, public parks, and shopping districts experience overwhelming daily crowds. This extreme concentration of human activity, constant loud talking, and commercial movement naturally generates an excessive level of background noise. Consequently, this intense crowding transforms public areas into chaotic and stressful environments that destroy local peace. For instance, a recent report on urban density showed that overcrowded mega-cities such as Tokyo and Mumbai face dangerous levels of public noise purely due to the sheer volume of citizens gathering in commercial centers every day.
>
> The primary solution to stop this problem is for governments to manage overpopulation through better urban planning. When state authorities intentionally develop new commercial zones, residential areas, and recreational parks far outside the crowded city center, they successfully spread out the population. This design effectively prevents too many citizens from gathering in a single public spot at the same time. Therefore, due to these spatial planning changes, central public areas become much quieter, calmer, and more comfortable for the community. For example, several modern cities namely, Barcelona and Seoul have designed decentralized neighborhoods with localized public squares. Following this structural change, citizen crowds have dropped significantly in main centers, returning peace and quiet to the streets.
>
> In conclusion, excessive city overpopulation causes severe noise in public areas by creating massive crowds. However, this issue can be successfully solved if governments use smart urban planning to distribute people across different zones.

*(321 words)*

**Analysis**

- *Cause:* overpopulation (migration from rural areas leads to crowded squares and parks, which leads to noise). The example is **Tokyo and Mumbai**.
- *Solution:* urban planning: new zones outside the centre spread the population out. The matching example is **Barcelona and Seoul**.
- The result sentences use *Consequently, …* and *Therefore, due to these … changes, …*

---

## Notes

- **Match causes and solutions.** Each solution should answer one of your causes (no jobs → create jobs; low pay → raise salaries).
- Say **who** implements the solution (*governments, employers, parents, schools*).
- The final example should show the solution **working**, with a fall in the problem (*"Eventually, health worker migration dropped significantly"*).
