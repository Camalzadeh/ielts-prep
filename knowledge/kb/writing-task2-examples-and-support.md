# Supporting Ideas with General and Statistical Examples

Every body paragraph in the teacher's plan ends with a **real-world example** (*"Provide a short, specific example (mentioning a country or organization) that proves your point"*). Elvira shared two "idea banks" so that students always have an example ready:

- **General examples**: a specific situation in a named country, for example *"Norway's prison system focuses heavily on rehabilitation rather than punishment."*
- **Statistical examples**: a number or percentage, for example *"Recidivism (reoffending) rates in many countries of released prisoners hover around 60-70%."*

Both handouts cover the **same 30 topics**, which are also the common IELTS Task 2 topics. The course's writing topic list was: work and careers, education and schooling, children and families, nature/environment/energy, culture/art/traditions, healthcare/health/sport, global challenges, cities and infrastructure, countryside and agriculture, government and the authorities.

---

## 1. Where the example goes in a paragraph

```
Topic sentence    → states the point
Explanation (x2)  → how / why it happens
Result            → the consequence for people or society
EXAMPLE           → a named country / organisation / study / statistic   ← here
```

The example comes **last** and **proves** the point you have just explained. It should not introduce a new idea.

## 2. Frames for introducing an example (from the teacher's model essays)

**General (country / organisation / scenario)**
- *A clear example of this is …, where …*
- *A clear example of this can be seen in …, where …*
- *A great example of this is seen in charity organizations like …, where …*
- *A prime example of this strategy is …, which successfully …*
- *A clear instance of this is …, where …*
- *A clear illustration of this is seen when … / occurs when …*
- *For example, several modern cities namely, … and … have …*

**Statistical (study / report / data)**
- *For instance, reports from the World Wildlife Fund (WWF) show that …*
- *For example, data from the National Center for Health Statistics shows that …*
- *For example, a study by Harvard University found that …*
- *For instance, the World Health Organization reported that children who … suffer from a forty percent higher rate of …*
- *To illustrate, a recent global study found that children raised in … have a 30% higher chance of …*
- *This situation is clearly shown by business research from companies like Gallup, which consistently proves that …*
- *For example, statistics published by National Geographic reveal that …*

More example linkers (from the *Linking Words* handout): *For example, For instance, To cite an example, To show an example, To illustrate, One clear example is*. See [linking-words.md](linking-words.md).

## 3. Worked example: turning an idea-bank line into a full paragraph ending

Idea-bank lines (Crime and Punishment):
- General: *"Norway's prison system focuses heavily on rehabilitation rather than punishment."*
- Statistic: *"Recidivism (reoffending) rates in many countries of released prisoners hover around 60-70%."*

Used at the end of a solution paragraph (*the best way to reduce crime is rehabilitation*):

> *…When prisoners receive education and job training, they are far less likely to return to crime after release. **A clear example of this can be seen in Norway, where the prison system focuses heavily on rehabilitation rather than punishment.** By contrast, **in many countries where prisons only punish, reoffending rates hover around 60–70%**.*

Combining a **general** and a **statistical** example (positive case vs. negative case) makes the paragraph especially convincing.

## 4. Tips

- **Name something.** "Some countries" is weak. "Finland", "the WHO" and "Harvard University" are strong.
- Keep the example **short**: one sentence, at most two.
- The example must **match** the point. In solution paragraphs, show a country where the solution **worked** (*"…migration dropped significantly"*).
- Examiners assess your **language and argument**, not whether a statistic is precisely correct. Still, keep numbers **plausible** and use cautious wording (*approximately, nearly, more than, around*). The teacher's own models use rounded, plausible figures attributed to well-known bodies. *(General exam advice.)*
- Learn **2–3 examples per topic** from the lists below rather than all of them.

#### Media and Advertising

- Advertising in India often uses Bollywood stars to increase brand credibility.

#### Transport and Infrastructure

#### Public Services

#### Science and Innovation

#### Immigration

#### Gender Roles

#### Social Issues

#### Sport and Leisure

- Hiking and nature trails in New Zealand attract tourists and locals alike.
- In Europe, cycling is both a leisure activity and a mode of transport.
- In the United States, eSports leagues are growing rapidly in popularity and sponsorship.

#### Law and Order

- In many European nations, drug laws are shifting from punitive to rehabilitative models.
- Australia has strict gun control laws which are often cited as successful in reducing gun violence.
- Police brutality in certain regions leads to calls for reform and accountability.

#### Animal Rights

- Wildlife trafficking in Africa threatens species like elephants and rhinos.
- Many European countries have laws banning fur farming.

- Silicon Valley tech firms sometimes hire high-school dropouts if they have proven coding skills.
- In many trades like plumbing or carpentry, apprenticeships provide hands-on learning that theory-based courses cannot match.
- Some graduates in India with degrees find better opportunities abroad because they lack practical internships.
- In Australia, many employers value work portfolios and certifications over formal academic transcripts.

#### Freedom of Speech

- Journalists in Russia often face serious repercussions for criticizing the government.
- Universities in the UK sometimes ban speakers whose views are deemed too extreme.

## 6. Statistical examples by topic (teacher's handout, verbatim)

#### Education

- Nearly 40% of students globally report that school curriculums do not match the skills required by employers.
- In many countries, over 30% of children drop out before secondary school due to financial constraints.
- A survey showed that 85% of parents believe standardized testing is an inadequate measure of students’ abilities.
- About 50% of universities now offer online courses alongside traditional degree programs.
- In recent years, spending on higher education has increased by more than 25% in developing nations.

#### Technology

- Over 70% of households in developed countries now have smart assistants like Alexa or Google Home.
- Approximately 60% of teens report feeling socially isolated despite heavy use of social media.
- The number of industrial robots in manufacturing has grown by 150% in the past decade.
- Globally, the internet penetration rate has reached nearly 65% of the population.
- More than 80% of employees believe that automation will significantly change their job in the next five years.

#### Environment

- The Earth has lost more than 30% of its forests in just the last fifty years.
- Carbon emissions per capita in many industrialized countries remain above 10 metric tons annually.
- Over 1 billion tons of plastic waste are produced globally every year.
- Sea levels have risen by about 8-9 inches since 1880, according to climate scientists.
- More than 40% of wildlife species are threatened by habitat destruction and climate change.

#### Health

- Obesity rates have doubled since 1980 in more than 70 countries worldwide.
- Mental health disorders affect about 1 in 4 people at some point in their lives.
- More than 80% of health care costs in many nations are spent on chronic (long-term) diseases.
- Life expectancy has increased by approximately 5 years globally over the past two decades.
- In low-income countries, over 50% of deaths are linked to preventable conditions such as poor sanitation and lack of clean water.

#### Crime and Punishment

- Some cities have seen violent crime rates drop by up to 30% following community policing programs.
- Recidivism (reoffending) rates in many countries of released prisoners hover around 60-70%.
- Over 90% of inmates in certain prisons report inadequate access to rehabilitation programs.
- The cost of incarcerating one prisoner often exceeds what many governments spend per student in public schools.
- In some jurisdictions, crime rates increase by 20-25% in areas with high youth unemployment.

#### Globalization

- Since 2000, international trade has more than tripled under globalization.
- Over 50% of all published scientific research now involves collaboration between researchers in multiple countries.
- More than 70% of people in urban areas consume products made abroad.
- Incoming foreign direct investment (FDI) has increased by 40-50% in developing countries in the last decade.
- Over 100 million people live outside their country of birth, reflecting rising migration linked to global economic integration.

#### Culture and Tradition

- In many multicultural nations, more than 60% of young people report that they feel less connected to traditional customs.
- Tourism has led to the commercialization of local festivals in over 25 countries worldwide.
- Languages are disappearing at a rate of one every two weeks globally.
- Traditional crafts have declined by more than 40% in some regions because of modern manufacturing.
- Over 70% of indigenous communities say their cultural heritage is threatened by urban expansion.

#### Government and Politics

- In recent elections, voter turnout has dropped below 50% in numerous democratic countries.
- Government spending on welfare programs has increased by 35% in many developed economies over the past 20 years.
- Trust in political institutions in several nations has fallen to under 30% according to recent polls.
- More than 80% of citizens in some countries believe their governments should do more to regulate social media.
- Corruption perception indices show that over 50% of countries suffer from moderate to high levels of perceived corruption.

#### Economy and Money

- Global inequality is increasing: the top 1% of households own more than 40% of global wealth.
- Inflation rates in many countries have exceeded 5% annually in recent years.
- Small businesses account for nearly 60-70% of employment in many economies.
- The gig economy has grown by over 20% in terms of workforce share in developed nations.
- More than 30% of households report being unable to save money monthly in some countries.

#### Work and Employment

- Remote work now accounts for over 25% of all jobs in certain sectors post-pandemic.
- Unemployment among youth (ages 15-24) in many regions is above 15-20%.
- More than 50% of workers believe their jobs could be automated within the next 10 years.
- Gig-economy workers—like delivery drivers and freelancers—make up 10-15% of the workforce in many large cities.
- Over 70% of employees say work-life balance is more important than salary.

#### Media and Advertising

- Internet advertising revenue surpassed $200 billion globally in recent years.
- Approximately 60% of consumers admit that targeted ads influence their purchasing decisions.
- Over 70% of news content consumed online is seen via social media.
- In many countries, more than 80% of households have access to streaming services.
- Ad spending on children’s programming has doubled in some markets in the last decade.

#### Family and Relationships

- In many societies, over 40% of marriages end in divorce.
- More than 30% of households are now single-parent families in certain metropolitan areas.
- Co-habitation (living together without marriage) has increased by over 50% in the last 20 years in western countries.
- Over 60% of adults say balancing work and family is difficult.
- In aging societies, more than 20% of the population is over 65, increasing demands for elder care.

#### Transport and Infrastructure

- Traffic congestion costs large cities upwards of $100 billion per year in lost productivity.
- Public transport usage has declined by 10-15% in some regions due to increased private car ownership.
- Over 40% of global energy consumption is used for transportation.
- The percentage of roads in developing countries that are paved remains under 50% in many rural areas.
- Rail freight volume has increased by over 20% in the past decade in many industrialized nations.

#### Public Services

- In many cities, police response times exceed 10 minutes in over 30% of emergencies.
- Public library usage has dropped by more than 40% in the digital age.
- More than 50% of hospitals in rural areas are understaffed.
- Over 25% of schools lack basic sanitation facilities in some developing countries.
- Less than 20% of the population in certain regions have reliable access to clean water.

#### Urbanization and Housing

- In many countries, urban populations are growing at rates of 2-4% annually.
- More than 30% of city residents live in informal settlements or slums in some megacities.
- Housing prices have risen by 50-100% in major capitals over the last decade.
- Nearly 40% of urban dwellers commute more than one hour to work each day.
- Vacancy rates for affordable housing often drop below 5% in large metropolitan areas.

#### Science and Innovation

- R&D investment globally has increased by over 20% in recent years.
- Over 60% of people trust scientific consensus on climate change.
- Patent applications have doubled in many countries over the past decade.
- Many start-ups in biotech have grown at over 30% annually.
- Advances in medical science have increased survival rates for many cancers by over 15% in recent decades.

#### Immigration

- The number of international migrants has more than doubled since the 1990s.
- Remittances from migrant workers amount to over $700 billion globally each year.
- In many countries, immigrants now make up more than 20% of the workforce.
- Public opinion polls show 50-60% support stricter immigration policies in several Western nations.
- Brain drain is a concern as over 30% of tertiary graduates emigrate from their home country in some developing nations.

#### Gender Roles

- Women’s participation in the workforce has risen above 50% in many OECD countries.
- In over 40% of households, men now take on more childcare responsibilities compared to twenty years ago.
- Pay gaps persist: women often earn 20-30% less than men for the same work in many regions.
- More than 70% of people agree that gender roles are becoming more flexible in modern societies.
- About 25% of leadership positions in major corporations are occupied by women in many countries (some less, some more).

#### Social Issues

- Income inequality has worsened: the share of income held by the top 10% has increased by several percentage points in many countries.
- In many cities, homelessness has increased by 20-25% over the past decade.
- Over 30% of the population in some nations live below the poverty line.
- Mental health stigma still remains: up to 50% of those with mental illness do not seek help.
- Discrimination based on race or ethnicity remains a factor in employment decisions for more than 40% of minorities in certain countries.

#### Art and Music

- Music streaming has grown over 300% worldwide in the past five years.
- Public funding for arts has dropped by more than 20% in many countries during financial austerity.
- Sales of traditional art crafts have fallen by over 30% in some cultures.
- More than 60% of young people attend concerts or festivals annually in many major cities.
- Digital art commissions have increased by over 50% in recent years.

#### Food and Diet

- Fast food consumption has grown by 25-30% among adolescents in developed countries.
- Over 40% of adults in some nations are overweight or obese.
- The vegan and vegetarian population has increased by 5-10% per annum in many western countries.
- Sugar intake of average adult has risen above 90 grams/day in some regions, well beyond WHO recommendations.
- More than 50% of people report eating outside the home at least twice during the week.

#### Tourism

- International tourist arrivals exceeded 1.5 billion globally in a recent year.
- In many destinations, tourism accounts for over 10−15% of national GDP.
- Overtourism causes visitor numbers to exceed twice the carrying capacity in some fragile ecosystems.
- Cultural heritage sites in more than 30 countries are under threat due to tourist traffic.
- Tourism employment contributes to over 20% of jobs in many developing islands and small economies.

#### Sport and Leisure

- More than 50% of people in many countries engage in sports at least once a week.
- Revenue from professional sports leagues has increased by over 100% in the last decade.
- Leisure industries contribute more than 15% to GDP in many developed nations.
- Participation in individual sports has grown by 25% compared to team sports among youth.
- Screen-based leisure (e.g. gaming, streaming) now takes up over 20 hours/week for many adolescents.

#### Law and Order

- In some cities, there are fewer than 5 police officers per 10,000 inhabitants, correlating with higher crime.
- Over 70% of citizens believe the legal system needs major reform.
- Cases of wrongful conviction, though rare, have increased due to flawed forensic methods.
- The cost of law enforcement takes up significant proportion of government budgets, sometimes exceeding 10%.
- Many jurisdictions report more than 20% of crimes go unsolved annually.

#### Animal Rights

- Over 100 million animals are used in laboratory testing each year worldwide.
- Wildlife populations have declined by 60% on average since 1970.
- More than 50% of consumers express concerns over animal cruelty when choosing products.
- Zoos now house fewer than 5% of endangered species in a way that contributes to their conservation in some regions.
- Legislation protecting animal welfare has been adopted by over 70% of countries, though enforcement is often weak.

#### Consumerism

- Average consumer debt in many countries has risen more than 20% in the past five years.
- Over 60% of people believe advertisements persuade them to buy unnecessary goods.
- Households spend nearly 30–40% of their income on non-essential items in some economies.
- Fast fashion contributes to 10% of global carbon emissions annually.
- The number of new consumer products released each year has grown by 50% in many developed markets.

#### Climate Change

- Global surface temperature has risen about 1.2°C since the late 19th century.
- Annual greenhouse gas emissions exceed 50 billion metric tons globally.
- Arctic ice extent has decreased by more than 40% in summer months over recent decades.
- Sea level is rising by about 3.3 mm/year on average.
- Over 90% of climate scientists agree that climate change is primarily caused by human activity.

#### Education vs Experience

- In many job ads, 80% list a degree as essential but only 30% mention specific work experience.
- Employers in some industries report that 60% of new graduates lack necessary hands-on skills.
- Over the past decade, internships and apprenticeships have increased by 40% in certain sectors.
- Some companies give preference to experienced workers, even if they lack formal qualifications, in over 25% of hiring decisions.
- Job-market data shows that graduates with practical training earn 20-30% more in their first years than those without.

#### Freedom of Speech

- Polls show 75% of citizens in democracies believe freedom of speech must be defended, even for unpopular ideas.
- Yet in some nations, about 40% of media outlets are state-controlled.
- Social media platforms remove posts for violating policies in over 1 million instances per year.
- Many universities globally (over 50%) have policies limiting controversial speeches.
- Legal cases show that in some countries, freedom of expression is restricted for more than 60% of political dissidents.

#### Censorship

- In some countries, more than 30% of books published are banned or censored in some form.
- Governments or platforms block access to websites in regions affecting over 1 billion people.
- Over 50% of social media users say they have self-censored their opinions online.
- Artistic works are often edited by regulatory boards: for example, 40% of films submitted for review require cuts.
- More than 25 countries have laws that restrict political satire or criticism.
