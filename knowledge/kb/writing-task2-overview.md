## 1. The task at a glance

| | Task 2 |
|---|---|
| Time | about **40 minutes** (standard IELTS) |
| Minimum length | **250 words** (standard IELTS). Below 250 words loses marks. |
| Weight | Task 2 counts for **about twice as much** as Task 1 in the Writing band, so never run out of time for it. |
| Teacher's model length | All of the teacher's model essays are **300–350 words** (for example 300, 312, 315, 317, 318, 321, 325, 334, 345 and 350 words). Aim for about **280–320** in the exam. |
| Paragraphs | **4 paragraphs**: Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, Conclusion. Every handout in the course uses this plan. |

**Suggested 40-minute split** (general exam advice, not from the handouts): 5 min to read the question, decide the type and plan both body paragraphs, 30 min to write, 5 min to check grammar, articles and word count. In the course's trial exams, Task 1 and Task 2 were written together in one session with Listening and Reading (about 3 hours in total).

## 2. Identify the essay type from the question wording

Read the **instruction line** (the last sentence or two). The background statement only gives the topic.

| If the question says… | Essay type | What you must do |
|---|---|---|
| *Do you agree or disagree?* / *To what extent do you agree or disagree (with this statement/opinion)?* | **Opinion** | Take one side clearly and give 2 reasons. |
| *Is this a positive or negative development?* / *…trend?* | **Positive / negative development** | Say if it is positive or negative and give 2 reasons. |
| *Discuss both views and give your opinion.* / *Discuss both these views and then give your own opinion.* / *Discuss both sides of this argument and give your own opinion.* | **Discussion** | One paragraph for the view you **disagree** with, then one for the view you **agree** with. |
| *What are the advantages and disadvantages?* / *Discuss the advantages and disadvantages.* | **Advantages / disadvantages** | One paragraph of advantages and one of disadvantages, the **same number** of each. No need to choose a side. |
| *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?* / *Do you think the advantages of this policy outweigh the disadvantages?* / *To what extent do the benefits … outweigh the disadvantages?* | **Outweigh** | Choose the stronger side. The stronger side gets the longer paragraph (two points), and the weaker side gets one point. |
| *What problems does this cause? What measures could be taken to solve them?* / *Discuss the problems and suggest ways…* | **Problem / solution** | Body 1 covers the problem(s) and Body 2 the solution(s). |
| *What are the causes of this? What effects can be seen as a result of this?* | **Cause / effect** | Body 1 covers the cause(s) and Body 2 the effect(s). |
| *What are the causes of this problem? What can be done to deal with / solve this?* | **Cause / solution** | Body 1 covers the cause(s) and Body 2 the solution(s). |
| Two **different** questions, e.g. *What are the reasons for this? Is this a positive or a negative development?* / *How important is…? Do you think…?* / *Do you agree or disagree? What is the best way…?* | **Direct question (two-part)** | Body 1 answers question 1 and Body 2 answers question 2. |

> **Tip:** Cause/effect, cause/solution and problem/solution are also two-question essays. They have their own fixed plans because they come up so often. Any other pair of questions uses the direct-question plan. The teacher labelled *"More and more people nowadays visit well-known places to take photographs of themselves… Why do you think this is happening? Is it a positive or a negative trend?"* as a **DIRECT QUESTION ESSAY**.

## 3. The universal 4-paragraph plan

Every essay type in the course is built from the same blocks. Only the *content* of the two body paragraphs changes.

| Paragraph | Length | Contents |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 2–3 sentences | **Sentence 1: Paraphrase the topic** ("Rewrite the topic statement in your own words using synonyms.") **Sentence 2: Thesis statement**: clearly state your position or the main points you will discuss (your opinion and two reasons, the main cause and main solution, and so on). |
| **Body paragraph 1** | 5–6 sentences | **Topic sentence**, then **Explanation** (2 sentences: "Go deeper into detail"), then **Result** ("Show the direct consequence or outcome"), then **Real-world example** ("Provide a short, specific example (mentioning a country or organization) that proves your point.") |
| **Body paragraph 2** | 5–6 sentences | The same pattern for the second point, side or question. |
| **Conclusion** | 1–2 sentences | **Restate / summarise** your position and the key points in new words. *"Reconfirm your final stance in a new way without introducing new info."* |

### The body paragraph formula (TEER)

From the teacher's cause/solution and problem/solution handouts:

1. **Sentence 1: Topic sentence**: state the main reason, problem, solution or point.
2. **Sentences 2 and 3: Explanation**: explain *how* or *why*. Go deeper into detail.
3. **Sentence 4: The result**: show the direct consequence for society, or the positive change.
4. **Sentence 5: Real-world example**: a short, specific example that names a **country or organization** (WHO, Harvard University, UNICEF, the EU, Gallup and so on).

Some model paragraphs make **two points** in one body paragraph (*"The primary reason… The second major cause is…"*). That is the way to reach 300+ words.

### A 5-paragraph variant (general advice)

The course handouts never use a 5-paragraph plan. If you have **three** clear points (for example, two reasons for your opinion plus a concession), you *can* write Introduction, Body 1, Body 2, Body 3 (concession and rebuttal) and Conclusion. In 40 minutes, though, the 4-paragraph plan with longer, better-developed body paragraphs is safer. It is what the teacher's 300–350-word models do.

## 4. Introduction frames (taken from the teacher's model essays)

**Sentence 1: paraphrase the topic**

- *In today's world, many people choose to …*
- *These days, many parents …* / *Nowadays, the increasing popularity of … has led to …*
- *It is common for contemporary children to …*
- *An increasing number of … are …* / *An increasing amount of … is severely affecting …*
- *All over the world, … has become much more common.*
- *In many countries around the world, the number of … is increasing rapidly.*
- *It is undeniably true that …*
- *The growing trend of … represents a major shift in how modern society operates today.*
- *The trend of … has become increasingly common in modern society.*
- *In the contemporary digital era, the traditional practice of … is rapidly declining.*
- *It is often argued that …, whereas others contend that …* (discussion)

**Sentence 2: thesis**

| Type | Frame |
|---|---|
| Opinion | *I disagree with the idea that …, because …* / *I completely agree with this perspective. This issue leads to … because …, while …* |
| Positive / negative | *I completely agree that this change is an entirely positive development because it … and …* |
| Discussion | *While …, I believe that … by … and …* |
| Advantages / disadvantages | *While this practice offers clear advantages, such as …, it also introduces significant drawbacks that warrant careful consideration.* |
| Outweigh | *I firmly believe that the drawbacks of this practice, specifically regarding … and …, far outweigh the single advantage of …* / *While this … increases …, I believe the benefits of … and … far outweigh this single drawback.* |
| Cause / solution | *This essay will examine how … cause(s) this problem, and explain why government action is the most realistic solution to this global issue.* |
| Cause / effect | *This trend is mainly caused by … . Ultimately, … can lead to …* / *This trend is mainly driven by …, and it results in … as well as …* |
| Problem / solution | *This worrying trend has created serious … problems across the globe, making it absolutely necessary for … to …* / *While this trend causes major problems with …, the most effective solution is for governments to …* |
| Direct question | *This shift is primarily driven by …, and I consider this to be a highly positive development because …* / *This essay will explain why I completely disagree with …, and argue that the most effective way to … is …* |

## 5. Body paragraph frames

**Topic sentences**

- *To begin with, … plays a massive role in …* / *Furthermore, …*
- *First, … Second, …* (positive/negative essays)
- *The primary reason why … is …* / *The second major cause is …*
- *The main cause behind the rise in … is …*
- *The primary solution is for governments to …* / *The second step is for … to …*
- *The effects of this trend are mostly negative for …*
- *One prominent merit of … is its ability to …* / *One of the most significant advantages of … is …*
- *Conversely, an oversupply of … can severely hinder …* / *However, the widespread adoption of … is not without drawbacks.*
- *On the one hand, many people believe that …* / *On the other hand, I agree with the perspective that …*

**Explanation and result**

- *When …, …* (cause and effect inside one sentence)
- *This means that …* / *This lack of … directly impedes …*
- *As a result, …* / *As a result of these …, …* / *Consequently, …* / *As a consequence of …, …*
- *This allows … to …* / *which naturally leads to …* / *leaving them with no choice but to …*
- *Therefore, due to these … changes, …*

**Examples** (always name a country, organisation or study)

- *For instance, reports show …* / *For example, data from the … shows that …*
- *A clear example of this is …* / *A clear example of this can be seen in …*
- *A great example of this is seen in …* / *A prime example of this … is seen in …*
- *To illustrate, a recent global study found that …*
- *This situation is clearly shown by business research from …*
- *A clear illustration of this is seen when …* / *A clear instance of this is …*

## 6. Conclusion frames

- *In conclusion, while …, I strongly believe that …* (opinion)
- *In conclusion, … is undoubtedly the primary threat to … .* (opinion, agree)
- *In conclusion, the transition toward … is a thoroughly beneficial change for …* (positive/negative)
- *In conclusion, although …, … . Ultimately, I maintain that …* (discussion)
- *In conclusion, while … can serve as a catalyst for …, it simultaneously …* (advantages/disadvantages)
- *… mean that the disadvantages clearly outweigh the advantages.* / *However, this disadvantage is heavily eclipsed by …* (outweigh)
- *In conclusion, a combination of … and … causes … . However, this issue can be solved if governments …* (cause/solution)
- *In conclusion, … are the main reasons why … . This problem not only … but also …* (cause/effect)
- *In conclusion, … are the main impacts of … today. Overcoming this global problem requires immediate, combined efforts from …* (problem/solution)
- *In conclusion, the shift away from … is a direct consequence of … . Ultimately, this change is highly beneficial because …* (direct question)

## 7. Checklist before you hand in

- [ ] Did I answer **every part** of the question (both questions in a two-part essay)?
- [ ] Is my **position clear** in the introduction *and* the conclusion (opinion, discussion, outweigh, positive/negative and direct-question types)?
- [ ] Does each body paragraph have **one clear topic sentence**, an explanation, a result and a **named real-world example**?
- [ ] Did I use linking words naturally (*Furthermore, Consequently, For instance, However*) and not in every sentence?
- [ ] **250+ words** (aim for about 300)?
- [ ] No new ideas in the conclusion.
- [ ] Articles (*a/an/the*), subject–verb agreement, plurals, and the spelling of the topic words.

These Task 2 questions came up in real or trial exams that Elvira's students sat in 2026:

|---|---|
| In many countries, there is a rising rate of juvenile delinquency because more young mothers work and therefore spend less time caring for their children. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Opinion |
| Some people believe that drivers should take a driving test every year. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Opinion |
| In many countries, modern buildings are changing the character and appearance of towns and cities. Some people believe that countries should protect their cultural identity by insisting that all new buildings are built in a traditional style. To what extent do you agree or disagree with this opinion? | Opinion |
| Money should be spent on creating new public buildings such as museums or town halls rather than renovating the existing ones. To what extent do you agree or disagree? | Opinion |
| Big salary is more important than job satisfaction. Do you agree or disagree? | Opinion |
| Some people believe that it is possible for a country to be economically successful and have a clean environment. Others argue that these two objectives are not compatible. Discuss both the views and give your opinion. | Discussion |
| Some people argue that it is useful to do research into family history… However others believe that it is better to think more about the present and future generations. Discuss both views, give your opinion. | Discussion |
| Information technology is changing many aspects of our lives… To what extent do the benefits of information technology outweigh the disadvantages? | Outweigh |
| People often argue that health care services should not be used for making profit. Do the disadvantages of private healthcare outweigh its advantages? | Outweigh |
| As countries develop, more fast-food outlets move into the country. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages? | Outweigh |

| Each year, the crime rate increases. What are the causes of crime and what could be done to prevent this rise in criminal activity? | Cause / solution |
