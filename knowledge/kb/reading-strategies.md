# Reading strategies by question type

In the course, reading was practised through the Unit pages (Cambridge 10–11 passages, checked with
**ieltsdeal.com** explanations) and two to four engnovate tests per lesson, from Cambridge 11 up to
Cambridge 21 in the trial exams (see [Course timeline](course-timeline-and-resources.md)). The teacher's
method was **do the test, then read the explanation for every mistake**. The ieltsdeal pages she linked
show, for each question, the exact sentence in the passage that gives the answer. This page is the
strategy guide that goes with that practice. It is based on public IELTS guidance, since the
teacher's reading teaching happened in class and not in the chat.

## 1. The Academic Reading test

- 3 passages, 40 questions, **60 minutes, no extra transfer time** (on paper you write on the answer
  sheet within the hour).
- Passages get harder: aim for roughly **17 / 20 / 23 minutes**.
- Questions within one task usually follow the order of the passage (**except** matching headings,
  matching information and matching features).
- Answers must be spelled correctly and copied **from the passage**. Respect the word limit.

Approximate band conversion (Academic): 30/40 ≈ 7.0, 33 ≈ 7.5, 35 ≈ 8.0, 23 ≈ 6.0, 27 ≈ 6.5.

## 2. Method for every passage

1. **Skim the passage (2–3 min)**: title, first sentence of each paragraph, names, dates and numbers. Make a
   one-word note per paragraph.
2. **Read the questions**: underline the key words and decide what you are looking for (a name? a
   number? a claim?).
3. **Scan** for the key words or their **synonyms**. IELTS paraphrases almost everything, e.g.
   *elderly ↔ older people*, *rose ↔ increased*, *not compulsory ↔ optional*.
4. **Read the sentence before and after carefully**, then answer.
5. Never leave a blank: there is no penalty for a wrong answer.

## 3. Strategies by question type

### TRUE / FALSE / NOT GIVEN (facts) and YES / NO / NOT GIVEN (writer's views)
- **TRUE / YES**: the passage says the same thing in other words.
- **FALSE / NO**: the passage says the **opposite**. It directly contradicts the statement.
- **NOT GIVEN**: the passage says **nothing** about this specific point, or only part of it.
- Watch the **qualifiers** in the statement: *all, always, only, never, most, some, the first*. A
  passage saying "many" does not confirm "all".
- Don't use your own knowledge. If the passage doesn't say it, it is NOT GIVEN.
- Answers follow passage order, so if you can't find Q5, its answer lies between Q4 and Q6.
- Write the letters exactly as asked (Unit 3 key: *N, Y, NG*). Writing *TRUE* in a Y/N/NG task is marked wrong.

### Matching headings
- Read the paragraph's **first and last sentence** first; the heading describes the **main idea**, not a detail.
- A heading that repeats a word from the paragraph is often a **trap**. Look for meaning, not matching words.
- Do the easy paragraphs first and cross out used headings. There are more headings than paragraphs.

### Matching information ("Which paragraph contains…")
- Answers are **not** in order and a paragraph can be used more than once.
- Look for the *type* of information: *a reference to…*, *an example of…*, *a comparison…*, *a reason
  why…*. Do this after the other tasks on the passage, when you already know the text.

### Matching features (people / theories / dates)
- Scan for the names first and mark them in the passage, then read what each person says.
- The statements paraphrase the people's opinions, so check carefully.

### Matching sentence endings
- Grammar helps: the ending must complete the sentence grammatically **and** be correct according
  to the passage. Questions are in passage order.

### Summary / note / table / flow-chart completion
- Decide the part of speech for each gap (noun, adjective, verb form, plural?).
- With a **word box**, the options are paraphrases, so match the meaning. Without a box, copy the exact
  word(s) from the passage within the word limit.
- The summary usually covers one section of the passage. Find where it starts and work forward.

### Sentence completion / short-answer questions
- Answers come in order. Copy exact words; check spelling and the word limit (*NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS*).
- In Unit 4 (Reading Passage 2, "Second nature"), the answers are single nouns or noun phrases taken
  directly from the passage: *transformation, young age, optimism, skills, negative emotions*.

### Multiple choice
- Find the part of the passage first, then compare **every** option with the text. Wrong options often
  use words from the passage but change the meaning.
- For "choose TWO", the answers can come in either order.

### Diagram labelling
- Usually describes a process or machine in one or two paragraphs. Labels are nouns copied from the
  text, in the order of the description.

## 4. Time management

| Minute | Passage 1 | Passage 2 | Passage 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–17 | all questions | | |
| 17–37 | | all questions | |
| 37–60 | | | all questions + guess the rest |

- Give each question about 1.5 minutes. If you are stuck, guess, mark it, and move on.
- On the computer test, use the **highlight** and **notes** tools for key words (the app's reading
  practice supports highlighting too).

## 5. After each test

1. Check every wrong answer against an explanation site (the teacher used <https://ieltsdeal.com/>).
2. Find the sentence that proves the right answer and note the **paraphrase** you missed.
3. Track mistakes by question type. Most students lose marks on NOT GIVEN vs FALSE and on matching
   headings. Ask the app's AI analysis to break your mistakes down by type.
