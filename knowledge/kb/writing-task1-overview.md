# Writing Task 1 Overview (Academic)

In Academic Task 1 you describe **visual information**: a graph, chart, table, map or process. You write a short **report**. It is not an essay, and you give **no opinions** and no reasons that aren't shown in the data.

| | Task 1 |
|---|---|
| Time | about **20 minutes** ("You should spend about 20 minutes on this task", as printed on the task sheets) |
| Minimum length | **"Write at least 150 words."** The teacher's first rule was *MIN 150 WORDS*. The teacher's models are 157–200 words. Aim for **170–190**. |
| Weight | about **one third** of the Writing score (Task 2 counts double), so don't spend more than 20 minutes on it. |
| Instruction | *"Summarise the information by selecting and reporting the main features, and make comparisons where relevant."* |

**20-minute split** (general advice): 3 min to study the chart, find the main trends and extremes and group the data; 14 min to write; 3 min to check numbers, tenses and word count.

## 1. The 4-paragraph structure (teacher's board notes)

The teacher's notes from the first Task 1 lesson:

> **MIN 150 WORDS**
>
> **TASK + GRAPH**
>
> **INTRODUCTION**
> - 1 sentence
> - Paraphrase the task
>
> **OVERVIEW**
> - Overall,
> - 1-2 sentences
> - While/whereas
> - NO NUMBER/PERCENTAGE/YEAR
> - INCREASE/DECREASE
> - MAX/MIN
>
> **DETAIL PART**
> - two paragraphs
> - SHOW NUMBER/PERCENTAGE/YEARS

| Paragraph | Purpose | Rules |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Introduction** | Say what the visual shows | **One sentence**, paraphrasing the task question (see §2). |
| **2. Overview** | The "big picture" | Starts with **Overall,** · 1–2 sentences · use **while / whereas** to contrast · **no numbers, percentages or years** · mention the main **increase/decrease** trends and the **max/min** (highest/lowest) categories. |
| **3. Body paragraph 1** | Details for group A | Specific data: numbers, percentages, years. Compare. |
| **4. Body paragraph 2** | Details for group B | Specific data for the remaining categories. Compare. |

No conclusion is needed. The overview does that job, and it is the single most important paragraph for the Task Achievement score.

## 2. Introduction: paraphrase the question

*"The simplest way to write the introduction is by paraphrasing the task question using synonyms."*

**Common structure:** **The chart/graph + shows/illustrates + what + where + when**

| In the question | Paraphrase as |
|---|---|
| shows | illustrates / gives information on / depicts / describes (also: presents data on, details, indicates) |
| number of | quantity of |
| amount of | quantity of |
| percentage | rate / proportion |
| information | data |
| from 2005 to 2010 | between 2005 and 2010 (or: over the period from 2005 to 2010) |
| in 1985 and 1995 | in the years 1985 and 1995 |

**Examples from the handout**

| Task question | Paraphrased introduction |
|---|---|
| The line graph shows the percentages of Australian exports to four countries. | The line graph illustrates the proportion of Australian exports to four distinct countries, namely Japan, the United States, China, and India, over the period from 1990 to 2012. |
| The line graph shows the proportion of residents living in Australia in millions from different regions between 1976 and 2011. | The line graph presents data on the percentage of people residing in Australia who came from different parts of the world over the period from 1976 to 2011. |
| The chart below shows the annual pay (thousands of US dollars) for doctors and other workers in seven countries in 2004. | The bar chart illustrates how the occupational earnings for doctors and other workers varied across seven nations in 2004. |
| The charts show the main methods of transportation for people travelling to a university for work or study in 2004 and 2009. | The pie charts illustrate the percentage of students and staff utilizing different modes of transportation to travel to a university in 2004 and 2009. |

Tips: name the categories with *namely …* when there are only a few; replace the time phrase (*from X to Y* → *over the period from X to Y* / *over a twelve-year period*); never copy the question word for word.

## 3. The overview: what to include

**Formula:** *Overall,* + main trend(s) **while/whereas** + contrasting trend. Optionally a second sentence (*In addition, …*) about the highest/lowest category or a change in ranking.

Overview examples from the handout. Both versions are acceptable, and the second is more analytical:

**Line graph: Australian exports to four countries (1990–2012)**
1. *Overall, exports to China and the US increased, with China experiencing a dramatic rise and becoming the largest export destination by the end of the period. In contrast, exports to Japan declined steadily, while India showed a gradual upward trend despite a slight fall at the end.*
2. *Overall, an upward trend was seen in the percentage of Australian goods exported to India and China, while the US and Japan witnessed an opposite trend over the period shown. In addition, at the beginning, Japan was the main importer of Australian goods, whereas in the last year China surpassed it and ranked first.*

**Line graph: residents of Australia by region (1976–2011)**
1. *Overall, whereas the proportion of citizens from the UK saw a downward trend, that of Asia and other regions experienced substantial upward trends over the mentioned period. Whilst the UK started at the highest point, it ended at the lowest one.*
2. *Overall, while the proportion of immigrants from the UK declined steadily throughout the period, the figures for Asia and other regions generally increased. As a result, despite some fluctuations, the total percentage ended slightly higher than its initial level.*

**Overview checklist**
- [ ] Starts with *Overall,*
- [ ] No numbers, no percentages, no years (*"at the beginning / by the end of the period / in the final year"* instead)
- [ ] General trend(s): up / down / stable / fluctuating
- [ ] Max/min: the biggest or smallest category, or a change of leader (*surpassed it and ranked first*)
- [ ] A contrast with *while / whereas / In contrast*

Overview formulas for **static** charts (one year, no trend), **maps** and **processes** are on their own pages.

## 4. Body paragraphs: how to group the data

Two body paragraphs, each with **specific data**. Choose the grouping that makes comparisons easy:

| Chart | Typical grouping (from the teacher's models) |
|---|---|
| Line graph | Body 1: the categories that **fell / started highest** · Body 2: the categories that **rose / started lowest** |
| Bar chart (time) | Body 1: the steady or mixed categories · Body 2: the category with the most dramatic change |
| Bar chart (static) | Body 1: group 1 (e.g. doctors) · Body 2: group 2 (e.g. other workers) |
| Pie charts | Body 1: the biggest segments · Body 2: *"Regarding the remaining categories…"* |
| Table | Body 1: the top or first rows · Body 2: the remaining rows (*"When it comes to…"*) |
| Two different charts | Body 1: the first chart · Body 2: the second chart |
| Map | Body 1: the first area or time period · Body 2: the second area or time period |
| Process | Body 1: first half of the stages · Body 2: second half |

In each body paragraph: **start with the most important figure**, give the **start and end values** with years, mention **peaks/lows**, and **compare** (*…which was $10,000 less than Italy*, *nearly twice as much as…*).

Two key distinctions decide your **tenses and vocabulary**:

1. **Dynamic (changes over time)**, for example a line graph or bar/pie/table data across years. Use trend language: *rose, fell, fluctuated, peaked*. See [writing-task1-language.md](writing-task1-language.md).
2. **Static (one point in time)**, for example one pie chart or a single-year table. Use comparison language: *accounted for, was twice as much as, the highest/lowest*.
3. **Past, present or future**: past dates use the past simple. Future projections (e.g. 2030, 2040) use **prediction structures** (*is expected to, is predicted to, will*).

All language (trend verbs and nouns, adverbs, approximations, fractions, prepositions of time, prediction structures) is collected in [writing-task1-language.md](writing-task1-language.md).

## 6. Common mistakes (from the students' class essays)

- Numbers in the overview: they belong in the body.
- An overview that just lists categories with no trend or comparison.
- Mixing tenses: past data needs the past simple (*rose, accounted for*), not the present.
- Describing every number. Select the main features: highest, lowest, start, end, peaks and crossings.
- Wrong subject: *"The number of books rose…"*, not *"Books rose…"*. Use *the figure for X* to avoid repetition.
- Misreading units (thousands, millions, %). In one real exam a student lost marks by misunderstanding the "high income / average income" labels. Read the axes and legend first.
