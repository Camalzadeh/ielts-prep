# Listening: strategies, map language and answer rules

The course did listening mostly as homework: one Unit in class (Cambridge 10–11 pages with audio)
plus two or three engnovate tests per lesson (see [Course timeline](course-timeline-and-resources.md)).
This page collects the teacher's listening material from the chat: the map vocabulary list, the
numbers & letters practice, her rulings on answer format, and the general test strategy that goes with it.

## 1. The test at a glance

| Part | Situation | Typical tasks | Answers |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Everyday conversation (booking, enquiry) | form / note / table completion | names, numbers, dates, postcodes, prices |
| 2 | Monologue on an everyday topic (tour, facility, event) | MCQ, **map / plan labelling**, matching | places, facilities, directions |
| 3 | Conversation in an academic context (students and tutor) | MCQ, matching, "choose TWO" | opinions, attitudes, agreement |
| 4 | Academic lecture | note / sentence completion | one word per gap, mostly nouns |

40 questions, about 30 minutes of audio, each recording heard **once**. The paper-based test adds 10 minutes
to transfer answers; the computer-delivered test gives 2 minutes to check.

## 2. Answer-format rules (teacher's rulings and answer keys)

**Articles.** A student asked (28 Jun, LATEST_EXAMS) whether an answer written *without the article*
(key: *a topic / one topic / 1 topic*) is marked wrong. The teacher's ruling:

> "Artiklsız səhv sayılmayacaq." (*Without the article it will not be counted wrong.*)

So for a key like *a term / one term*, writing **term** without *a* is fine. Do not add extra words
that break the word limit, though.

**Synonyms are not accepted if the meaning differs.** In the same question (Q27, key *a term / one term*),
the student wrote **12 weeks** because the speaker also said "12 weeks". The teacher: *"27 səhvdir"* (27 is
wrong). Write the word the key needs, the one that fits the gap grammatically and was given as the answer, not a
paraphrase or an equivalent amount.

**Rules you can see in the course answer keys (Units 1–3):**
- **Singular/plural alternatives** are both accepted when the gap allows either: *beach/beaches*,
  *power/powers*, *method/methods*, *animal/animals*, *photo card/photo cards*.
- **Possessive forms** may be accepted both ways: *dentist/dentist's*.
- **Numbers** are written as digits: *2020*, *429*, *19*.
- **Codes and postcodes** must be copied letter by letter: *GT82LC*.
- **"IN EITHER ORDER"**: for "choose TWO letters" questions (e.g. 11 & 12 → A, C), the two letters can
  go in either box.
- Reading-style keys (Y / N / NG, T / F / NG) use the letters, not words.

**Standard IELTS rules to follow:**
- Obey the word limit: *ONE WORD ONLY*, *NO MORE THAN TWO WORDS AND/OR A NUMBER*. Hyphenated words
  count as one word; a number written in digits counts as a number, not a word.
- Spelling must be correct. British and American spellings are both accepted.
- Capital letters do not matter in the paper test, but using **CAPITALS** for all answers avoids any
  doubt.
- Write units only if they are not printed next to the gap (*£ ____* → just the number).

## 3. Numbers & letters

Part 1 always tests spelling of names and addresses, phone numbers, dates and prices. The teacher posted
three practice videos on 21 July (*Numbers & letters practice*):

1. <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JiagO6-PWhM&t=15s>
2. <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iNQ8vtM2Vu4&t=0s>
3. <https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=n9E-1kURLEk>

Common traps:
- **Letters** that sound alike: *A/E/I*, *B/P/V*, *G/J*, *M/N*, *S/F*, *Q/U*. Listen for "double" (*double L*
  = *LL*).
- **Numbers**: *thirteen / thirty*, *fifteen / fifty* (the stress is on the end in *-teen*). "Oh" = zero
  in phone numbers; "double five" = 55; "triple" = three of the same.
- **Corrections**: the speaker gives one number, then changes it ("sorry, it's actually…"). The
  last version is the answer.
- **Dates**: write them as you would in British English (*12 March* or *March 12* are both fine).
- **Prices**: watch out for "it *was* £20 but now it's £18".

## 4. Map and plan labelling — the teacher's word list

Posted on 24 July as **"TERMS USED IN MAP QUESTIONS"**. Learn these until you recognise them instantly;
map questions are usually in Part 2, and the speaker moves around the map in order.

| Directions | Position | Movement / route |
|---|---|---|
| North / east / west / south | In the centre of / in the middle of | Junction |
| Northeast, Northwest | Below / above | Leads off |
| Southeast, Southwest | Outside / inside | Dead end |
| Northside, Southside | Right-hand side / left-hand side | Runs alongside |
| Eastside, Westside | Next by / next to | Enter via |
| To your right | Alongside, adjoining | Turn right |
| | Behind, between | Along, go straight |
| | Corner | A little beyond, beyond |
| | | Before you get to |
| | | Just past |

### Phrases used in map questions (teacher's examples)

- The classroom is **located in front of** the kitchen.
- The dining room **faces** the bedroom.
- The stadium is **centrally located** on the college campus.
- The classroom is **situated behind** the stadium.
- The restroom is **positioned between** the gym and reception.
- The seating area is **adjacent to** the gym.
- **Upon entering** the college campus, the admission department is **on the right**.
- **To the left side of** the school, there is a water cooler.
- **Go through the entrance, turn left**, and the classroom will be **straight ahead**.
- There is an open area **just past** the stadium.
- **Head straight to** the conference room next to the canteen, and **on your left**, you'll see two gyms.
- The shopping plaza **directly to the south and north of** the hospital is near the school.
- The park is **just beyond** the first row of houses **to the east**.
- The garden is located **just southwest of** the high school.
- The theatre is **a bit west of** the primary school.
- There is a post office **on the east side of** the college.
- The garden is **across from** a departmental store.
- A new school is planned to be built **across the road from** the hospital at point F.
- The library is **on the same side of the road as** the school.

The same vocabulary is used to describe maps in Writing Task 1, see [Task 1 Map](writing-task1-map.md).

**Strategy for map questions**
1. In the reading time, find the *you are here* point / entrance and the compass.
2. Name what is already labelled; the speaker will use those labels as landmarks.
3. Questions follow the order of the talk. If you miss one, move on to the next landmark.
4. Listen for prepositions more than nouns: *just past*, *opposite*, *before you get to*.

## 5. General strategy by question type

- **Form/note/table completion**: predict the type of word for every gap (number? name? plural noun?)
  before the audio. Check grammar: the word must fit the sentence.
- **Multiple choice**: underline the key difference between options. All options are usually
  mentioned. The distractors are mentioned and then rejected ("I thought about…, but…").
- **Matching (Part 3)**: the options are often paraphrased opinions. Write a short key word next to
  each option.
- **Sentence completion (Part 4)**: the lecture follows the order of the notes. Headings in the notes
  tell you where you are. Answers are usually nouns heard exactly.
- **Choose TWO**: they may come in any order; listen for two clear "yes" moments.

## 6. Practice routine used in the course

1. Do the test on engnovate under exam conditions (one listening, no pausing).
2. Check answers, then **read the audioscript** for every mistake (the teacher posted audioscript links
   for each Unit: ieltstrainingonline / ieltsprogress).
3. Record why you got it wrong: spelling, plural, distractor, word limit, didn't hear.
4. For spelling mistakes, add the word to your vocabulary deck.

Your listening attempts and mistakes are stored by the app. Ask the AI analysis for a
pattern report after a few tests.
