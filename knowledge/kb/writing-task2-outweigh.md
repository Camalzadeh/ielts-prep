# Outweigh Essay

## How to recognise it

- *Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages (of this practice / development)?*
- *Do you think the advantages of this policy outweigh the disadvantages?*
- *To what extent do the benefits of … outweigh the disadvantages?*
- *Do the disadvantages of … outweigh its advantages?*

This is an **opinion** essay about advantages and disadvantages. You must decide **which side is stronger**. A plain [advantages/disadvantages essay](writing-task2-advantages-disadvantages.md) does not ask for this.

## Paragraph plan (teacher's handout, sentence by sentence)

| Paragraph | Sentences |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | Paraphrase the question. **Thesis statement**: state which one outweighs the other. |
| **Main body paragraph 1: the stronger side** | Topic sentence, then explain why it is strong, then an example. Then a **second** topic sentence, explain why it is strong, and an example. *(two points)* |
| **Main body paragraph 2: the weaker side** | Sentence 10: topic sentence. Sentence 11: explain why it is **not strong**. Sentence 12: example. *(one point)* |
| **Conclusion** | Sentence 13: summary of the main points and restate your position. |

> **Key idea:** the stronger side gets **two** developed points (a longer paragraph). The weaker side gets **one** point, and you explain why that point is weak or limited. The essay's shape shows your answer before the reader even reaches the conclusion.

## Sentence frames from the models

| Purpose | Frame |
|---|---|
| Paraphrase | *It is common for contemporary … to devote a significant portion of their … to …* / *… plays a critical role in shaping …* |
| Thesis (disadvantages win) | *I firmly believe that the drawbacks of this practice, specifically regarding … and …, far outweigh the single advantage of …* |
| Thesis (advantages win) | *While this … strategy increases …, I believe the benefits of … and … far outweigh this single drawback.* |
| Stronger side, point 1 | *On the one hand, excessive … severely damages …* / *Separating … significantly improves …* |
| Stronger side, point 2 | *Furthermore, this … acts as a major barrier to …* / *Additionally, this … catalyzes the development of …* |
| Weaker side: topic | *On the other hand, … can serve as an accessible tool for …* / *On the other hand, … create the notable disadvantage of …* |
| Weaker side: why weak | *However, this benefit is weak because …* |
| Examples | *For instance, the World Health Organization reported that …* / *For example, a recent study by Harvard University tracked … and found that …* / *A clear instance of this is …, where …* |
| Conclusion | *… mean that the disadvantages clearly outweigh the advantages.* / *However, this disadvantage is heavily eclipsed by the immense rewards of …* |

## Model essay 1: Disadvantages outweigh

**Question:** *Today children spend a lot of their free time watching TV. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages of this practice?*

> It is common for contemporary children to devote a significant portion of their leisure time to watching television. I firmly believe that the drawbacks of this practice, specifically regarding physical health and social development, far outweigh the single advantage of information.
>
> On the one hand, excessive television viewing severely damages the physical well-being of young individuals. Sitting in front of a screen for extended periods promotes an inactive lifestyle, which eliminates physical movement and alters metabolism to cause severe, lifelong medical conditions. For instance, the World Health Organization reported that children who watch more than three hours of television daily suffer from a forty percent higher rate of childhood diabetes and chronic spinal posture deformities. Furthermore, this passive habit acts as a major barrier to healthy childhood socialization. When children prioritize screens over real-life interactions, they fail to practice vital interpersonal skills such as sharing, conflict resolution, and empathy. For example, a recent study by Harvard University tracked primary school pupils and found that those with heavy television habits were twice as likely to exhibit social anxiety and struggle to form meaningful friendships with classmates.
>
> On the other hand, television can serve as an accessible tool for global education and information. However, this benefit is weak because television remains a completely passive learning medium that lacks interactive engagement, and children often consume uninformative entertainment rather than educational content. For instance, global media statistics show that eighty percent of children under the age of twelve choose to watch fictional, fast-paced cartoons or commercial advertisements rather than educational nature programs like National Geographic, which ultimately nullifies the intellectual value of the medium.
>
> In conclusion, while television is a rich source of information, it should not replace active pastimes. The severe risks it poses to a child's physical health and interpersonal skills mean that the disadvantages clearly outweigh the advantages. Parents must therefore regulate screen time to ensure balanced development.

*(317 words)*

**Analysis**

- *Thesis:* drawbacks (physical health and social development) "far outweigh the single advantage of information".
- *Body 1 (stronger side, two points):* physical health (example: the **WHO**, 40% higher rate of diabetes), then socialisation (example: a **Harvard** study, twice as likely to have social anxiety).
- *Body 2 (weaker side, one point):* education/information. *"However, this benefit is weak because…"*, then the example that 80% of children choose cartoons over National Geographic.
- *Conclusion:* restates the position and adds a recommendation (*Parents must therefore regulate screen time…*).

## Model essay 2: Advantages outweigh

**Question:** *In many cities, planners tend to arrange shops, schools, offices and homes in specific areas and separate them from each other. Do you think the advantages of this policy outweigh the disadvantages?*

> Urban planning plays a critical role in shaping the daily lives of city residents. In many modern cities, authorities choose to segregate residential, commercial, and educational zones from one another. While this zoning strategy increases daily travel times for citizens, I believe the benefits of reduced pollution and organized infrastructure development across the city far outweigh this single drawback.
>
> Separating distinct city zones significantly improves the health and living conditions of residents. When industrial and commercial sectors are kept away from neighborhoods, localized air and noise pollution drop sharply. By keeping heavy machinery, factory emissions, and constant delivery truck traffic far from residential areas, families can breathe cleaner air and experience quiet, peaceful living environments. For example, residential districts in Singapore enjoy high air quality because manufacturing plants and heavy commercial hubs are restricted to specific industrial estates like Jurong. Additionally, this separation catalyzes the development of balanced infrastructure across the entire city. When functions are grouped, local governments can design highly specialized public transit, utilities, and green spaces tailored specifically to the unique density and demands of that district, rather than trying to fit conflicting services into a chaotic, mixed-use neighborhood. A clear instance of this is Curitiba, Brazil, where master planners built dedicated bus rapid transit lines connecting distinct structural zones; this allowed the government to maximize public transit efficiency, reduce total city gridlock, and modernize the whole municipality's economy simultaneously.
>
> On the other hand, zoning policies create the notable disadvantage of extended commuting times. When homes, schools, and offices are far apart, citizens must travel long distances every day to meet their basic needs. For instance, the car-centric layout of Houston, Texas, forces workers to spend hours sitting in traffic gridlock just to commute from suburban homes to downtown offices. This structural flaw relies heavily on personal vehicles, which ultimately causes severe traffic congestion and mental fatigue for commuters.
>
> In conclusion, segregating different urban functions introduces the undeniable challenge of longer and more exhausting daily commutes. However, this disadvantage is heavily eclipsed by the immense rewards of cleaner residential air and highly organized, efficient city-wide infrastructure development.

*(350 words)*

**Analysis**

- The thesis names the single drawback (longer travel times) and the two benefits (less pollution, organised infrastructure).
- Body 1 has **two** advantages with two examples: **Singapore/Jurong** and **Curitiba, Brazil**. Body 2 has **one** disadvantage with one example: **Houston, Texas**.
- The conclusion uses the strong verb *eclipsed*: "this disadvantage is heavily eclipsed by the immense rewards of…"

---

## Notes

- **Always answer the question directly.** Say which side outweighs the other in the introduction **and** the conclusion.
- The weaker-side paragraph should **admit** the point and then **limit** it (*However, this benefit is weak because…*). Don't just list it.
- Useful verbs: *outweigh, override, overshadow, eclipse, be offset by*.

## Practice questions (real exams shared by the teacher)

- Information technology is changing many aspects of our lives and now dominates our home, leisure and work activities. To what extent do the benefits of information technology outweigh the disadvantages?
- In the technological age, companies and organisations keep private information about people. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?
- People often argue that health care services should not be used for making profit. Do the disadvantages of private healthcare outweigh its advantages?
- As countries develop, more fast-food outlets move into the country. Do the advantages outweigh the disadvantages?
