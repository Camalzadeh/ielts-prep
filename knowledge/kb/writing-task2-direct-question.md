# Direct Question Essay (Two-Part / Double Question)

## How to recognise it

The background statement is followed by **two different direct questions**. They can be any combination:

| Combination | Example from the course |
|---|---|
| *Why…? + Positive or negative?* | *What are the reasons for this? Is this a positive or a negative development?* |
| *How important…? + Do you think…?* | *How important is it to celebrate such events? Do you think people spend too much money on these events?* |
| *Agree/disagree? + What is the best way…?* | *Do you agree or disagree? What is the best way to punish children?* |
| *What factors…? + Would it be better if…?* | *What factors do you think influence these decisions? Would it be better if more good news was reported?* (real exam) |
| *Why…? + Is X more important than Y?* | *Why do we need music? Is the traditional music of a country more important than the international music…?* (real exam) |

The teacher labelled *"More and more people nowadays visit well-known places to take photographs of themselves, without looking at the place. Why do you think this is happening? Is it a positive or a negative trend?"* a **DIRECT QUESTION ESSAY**.

(Cause/effect, cause/solution and problem/solution are also two questions, but they have their own pages.)

## Paragraph plan (teacher's "IELTS Task 2 Question Types: Two Questions (Double Questions)")

| Paragraph | Length | What to write |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 2–3 sentences | **Paraphrase the prompt**: rewrite the background statement/context using academic synonyms. **Outline statement (direct answer)**: write **one clear sentence that directly answers both questions** asked in the prompt. This acts as your thesis statement. |
| **Body paragraph 1: answer question 1** | 5–6 sentences | **Topic sentence**: clearly state the direct answer to the first question. **Explanation**: explain why or how this is the case. **Example/result**: a concrete, real-world example, or the logical outcome of this point. |
| **Body paragraph 2: answer question 2** | 5–6 sentences | **Topic sentence**: clearly state the direct answer to the second question. **Explanation**: deepen the point with academic supporting arguments. **Example/result**: a concrete, real-world example or logical outcome. |
| **Conclusion** | 1–2 sentences | **Summarise the main points**: restate your answers to both question 1 and question 2 using different vocabulary. |

> **Rule:** one body paragraph per question, **in the order the questions are asked**. The outline statement must answer *both* questions. Don't make the reader wait for the conclusion.

## Sentence frames from the models

| Purpose | Frame |
|---|---|
| Paraphrase | *In the contemporary digital era, the traditional practice of … is rapidly declining.* / *In modern society, people frequently …* / *In many cultures, … is considered a fundamental responsibility of …, yet … remain highly controversial.* |
| Outline (reason + evaluation) | *This shift is primarily driven by … and …, and I consider this to be a highly positive development because it … and …* |
| Outline (importance + opinion) | *While some critics argue that …, I believe that … is very important for … . However, the modern trend of … is caused by … rather than …* |
| Outline (opinion + best way) | *This essay will explain why I completely disagree with …, and argue that the most effective way to … is through …* |
| Body 1 (reason) | *The primary reason for the decline in … is …* |
| Body 1 (importance) | *Celebrating … is highly important because it … and …* |
| Body 1 (opinion) | *I firmly disagree with the view that …* |
| Body 2 (evaluation) | *I firmly believe that this transition toward … is a profoundly positive development. Firstly, … Secondly, …* |
| Body 2 (second answer) | *Nevertheless, despite the clear … of …, it is obvious that …* / *Instead of …, the best way to … is through …* |
| Examples | *For example, in modern universities worldwide, …* / *A clear example of this can be seen in …* / *For instance, a recent study by … showed that …* |
| Conclusion | *In conclusion, the shift away from … is a direct consequence of … . Ultimately, this change is highly beneficial because …* / *In conclusion, while …, … . Society must learn to …* |

## Model essay 1: Reasons + positive/negative

**Question:** *Fewer and fewer people today write by hand using a pen or pencil. What are the reasons for this? Is this a positive or a negative development?*

> In the contemporary digital era, the traditional practice of handwriting with a pen or pencil is rapidly declining. This shift is primarily driven by the ubiquity of digital devices and the demand for workplace efficiency, and I consider this to be a highly positive development because it enhances global communication speed and reduces paper consumption.
>
> The primary reason for the decline in manual writing is the rapid advancement and widespread accessibility of digital technology. In both educational and corporate environments, electronic devices such as laptops, smartphones, and tablets have replaced physical stationery. Typing on a keyboard or touchscreen is fundamentally faster and more efficient than writing by hand, allowing individuals to process and document information at an unprecedented pace. For example, in modern universities worldwide, students routinely utilize cloud-based note-taking applications like Notion or Microsoft OneNote during lectures. This allows them to synchronize their notes across multiple devices instantly and search through vast amounts of text in seconds, a feat that is entirely impossible with traditional notebooks.
>
> I firmly believe that this transition toward digital communication is a profoundly positive development. Firstly, the widespread adoption of typing significantly boosts productivity and streamlines professional workflows across global industries. Digital text can be instantly edited, formatted, and shared across the world, eliminating geographical barriers to collaboration. Secondly, moving away from paper-based documentation offers immense environmental benefits. For instance, large multinational corporations like Google and Amazon have implemented strict "paperless office" initiatives, shifting all internal documentation, contracts, and invoicing to secured digital clouds. This structural change saves millions of trees annually, drastically lowering the corporate carbon footprint and reducing industrial waste.
>
> In conclusion, the shift away from handwriting is a direct consequence of technological integration and the need for operational speed. Ultimately, this change is highly beneficial because it optimizes human productivity while actively contributing to global environmental conservation.

**Analysis**

- *Outline statement:* one sentence answers **both** questions: the reasons (digital devices, workplace efficiency) and the evaluation (positive, because of speed and less paper).
- *Body 1 (Q1, reasons):* the example is **Notion / Microsoft OneNote** in universities.
- *Body 2 (Q2, evaluation):* *Firstly…* (productivity), *Secondly…* (environment). The example is **Google and Amazon** paperless offices.

## Model essay 2: How important + do you think…

**Question:** *Some people spend a lot of money celebrating personal family events, such as weddings and birthdays. How important is it to celebrate such events? Do you think people spend too much money on these events?*

> In modern society, people frequently spend large amounts of money on celebrating personal family events, such as weddings and birthdays. While some critics argue that these expensive parties are simply a waste of money, I believe that celebrating these milestones is very important for keeping families close. However, the modern trend of overspending is caused by social pressure rather than real necessity.
>
> Celebrating major family milestones is highly important because it strengthens personal relationships and protects cultural traditions. Family gatherings act as essential social events that bring different generations together, helping people feel connected and supported in a busy world. Furthermore, the shared memories created during these events form the foundation of a family's unique identity. A clear example of this can be seen in traditional South Asian weddings, which typically last for several days and involve hundreds of guests. These gatherings are not just for entertainment; they act as important social structures that reinforce extended family connections, provide emotional support for the newlyweds, and ensure that cultural traditions are successfully passed down to younger generations.
>
> Nevertheless, despite the clear cultural value of these gatherings, it is obvious that modern spending has become way too high. Modern consumer habits, which are increased by visual social media platforms like Instagram, have changed what people expect by turning private milestones into public shows. As a result, celebrations are often turned into competitions to show off wealth and social status, forcing families to face serious financial debt for short-lived luxury. For instance, a recent study by the financial institution Barclays showed that a growing number of young couples in the United Kingdom take out high-interest personal loans to fund weddings that cost over thirty thousand pounds. This trend shows that people regularly risk their long-term financial security simply to get social approval and praise from others.
>
> In conclusion, while the celebration of personal family events plays an essential role in maintaining family unity and cultural heritage, the money spent on them has become artificially high. Society must learn to separate the true emotional value of these milestones from the commercial pressure to overspend.

**Analysis**

- Body 1 answers "How important?" (very important: relationships and traditions). The example is **South Asian weddings**.
- Body 2 answers "Too much money?" (yes, because of social pressure from Instagram). The example is a **Barclays** study of UK couples taking out loans.

## Model essay 3: Agree/disagree + best way

**Question:** *Parents should punish children physically when they make mistakes. Do you agree or disagree? What is the best way to punish children?*

> In many cultures, disciplining children is considered a fundamental responsibility of parenting, yet the methods used remain highly controversial. This essay will explain why I completely disagree with the use of physical punishment on children, and argue that the most effective way to correct bad behavior is through the removal of privileges and clear communication.
>
> I firmly disagree with the view that parents should physically punish their children when they make mistakes. Corporal punishment, such as hitting or spanking, is a flawed disciplinary method that causes severe psychological harm rather than teaching actual moral lessons. When a child is physically punished, they do not understand the underlying reason why their action was wrong; instead, they simply learn to fear the parent. This fear frequently leads to long-term emotional issues, such as anxiety, low self-esteem, and childhood depression. For example, a comprehensive global study published by UNICEF found that children who experienced physical punishment at home were twice as likely to develop aggressive behaviors and mental health struggles compared to those raised with non-violent discipline.
>
> Instead of physical force, the best way to punish and correct a child's behavior is through non-violent, behavioral consequences. The most effective modern strategy is the temporary removal of privileges, particularly access to digital entertainment. When a child makes a mistake, parents should confiscate their smartphones, tablets, or gaming consoles, and pair this action with a calm conversation explaining what they did wrong. This approach forces the child to reflect on their choices without damaging their emotional well-being. A clear example of this can be seen in modern educational programs across Scandinavia, where parents are trained to use "digital grounding" and positive reinforcement. Studies show that this method successfully teaches self-discipline, respects the child's dignity, and maintains a strong, trusting relationship between the parent and the child.
>
> In conclusion, physical punishment is a highly damaging practice that fails to teach children proper behavior and instead causes emotional trauma. Ultimately, the superior method of discipline is to enforce behavioral consequences, such as taking away privileges, while using clear communication to guide them toward better choices in the future.

**Analysis**

- The *"This essay will explain why… and argue that…"* outline is a safe, reusable thesis for any two-question prompt.
- Body 1 example: a **UNICEF** global study. Body 2 example: **Scandinavian** "digital grounding" programmes.

## Notes

- **Answer both questions equally.** A common mistake is writing a long Body 1 and a weak Body 2.
- Use the **wording of each question** in your topic sentences so the examiner can see which question you are answering (*The primary reason for…*, *…is highly important because…*).
- If one question asks for your opinion (*Do you think…? / positive or negative?*), state it firmly. *"I firmly believe…"* and *"I firmly disagree…"* are the teacher's choices.

## Practice questions (real exams shared by the teacher)

- More and more people nowadays visit well-known places to take photographs of themselves, without looking at the place. Why do you think this is happening? Is it a positive or a negative trend?
- An increasing number of people are choosing to have cosmetic surgery in order to improve their appearance. Why do people have operations to change the way they look? Do you think this is a positive or negative development?
- News editors decide what to broadcast on television and what to print in newspapers. What factors do you think influence these decisions? Would it be better if more good news was reported?
- There are many different types of music in the world today. Why do we need music? Is the traditional music of a country more important than the international music that is heard everywhere nowadays?
- Some children spend hours every day on smartphones. Why is this the case? Do you think this is a positive or negative development?
