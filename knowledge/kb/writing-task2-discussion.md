# Discussion Essay (Discuss Both Views + Opinion)

## How to recognise it

The question presents **two opposing views** ("Some people think… while others believe…") and ends with one of these instructions (as listed on the teacher's handout):

- *Discuss both views and give your opinion.*
- *Discuss both these views and then give your own opinion.*
- *Discuss both sides of this argument and give your own opinion.*

You must do **both**: discuss each view fairly **and** say which one you support.

## Paragraph plan (teacher's handout)

| Paragraph | What to write |
|---|---|
| **Introduction** | **Introduce both views**: briefly state the two opposing sides of the argument. **State your opinion**: clearly tell the reader which side you support. |
| **Body paragraph 1: the disagreed view** | **Topic sentence**: introduce the side you ultimately *disagree* with. **Explanation**: explain why people hold this view and the logic behind their thinking. **Example**: a specific, concrete example or study that illustrates this point. |
| **Body paragraph 2: the agreed view** | **Topic sentence**: introduce the side you *agree* with. **Explanation**: detail why this perspective is stronger, more logical or more beneficial. **Example**: a real-world example, statistic or scenario that supports this position. |
| **Conclusion** | **Restate your opinion**: reconfirm your final stance in a new way without introducing new info. |

> **Order matters:** write the view you **disagree** with first and the view you **agree** with second. The essay then builds towards your opinion, and the conclusion follows naturally from Body 2.

## Sentence frames (the teacher uses the same skeleton in both models)

| Part | Frame |
|---|---|
| Intro: both views | *It is often argued that …, whereas others contend that …* |
| Intro: opinion | *While … are vital / … provide unmatched …, I believe that … due to / by …* |
| Body 1 topic | *On the one hand, many people believe / maintain that …* |
| Body 1 explanation | *Proponents of this view maintain / emphasize that …* / *Consequently, …* / *Furthermore, …* |
| Body 1 example | *A clear example of this is …, where …* / *A clear example of this can be seen in …, where …* |
| Body 2 topic | *On the other hand, I agree with the perspective that …* |
| Body 2 explanation | *By …, … enable …* / *Additionally, …* |
| Body 2 example | *For instance, …, resulting in / allowing …* |
| Conclusion | *In conclusion, although …, … . Ultimately, I maintain that … because / as …* |

Useful verbs for reporting views: *argue, contend, maintain, believe, emphasize, claim, hold the view that.*

## Model essay 1

**Question:** *Some people think that the main benefit of international cooperation is in the protection of the environment, while others believe that businesses mainly benefit. Discuss both these views and give your own opinion.*

> It is often argued that the primary objective of international collaboration is to protect the natural environment, whereas others contend that commercial enterprises derive the greatest benefit. While global ecological agreements are vital, I believe that international cooperation yields the most substantial advantages for businesses by establishing stable economic frameworks and expanding consumer markets.
>
> On the one hand, many people believe that the most critical outcome of cross-border alliances is environmental conservation. Proponents of this view maintain that ecological crises, such as climate change and biodiversity loss, are inherently transboundary issues that cannot be resolved by any single nation. Consequently, synchronized global action is essential to implement effective long-term solutions. A clear example of this is the Paris Climate Agreement, an international treaty where signatory nations committed to specific emission reduction targets, thereby compelling governments to invest heavily in sustainable infrastructure and renewable energy sectors.
>
> On the other hand, I agree with the perspective that corporate entities are the principal beneficiaries of global diplomacy. Political cooperation between nations routinely leads to the reduction of trade barriers, such as tariffs and import quotas. By standardizing regulatory frameworks, international agreements enable multinational corporations to access foreign consumer bases, optimize supply chains, and utilize cost-effective labor and raw materials. For instance, the United States-Mexico-Canada Agreement (USMCA) eliminated major trade barriers across North America, allowing automotive manufacturers like General Motors to streamline their production lines across borders and substantially lower operational costs.
>
> In conclusion, although international partnerships play an indispensable role in addressing global environmental degradation, they serve as the foundational framework for global commerce. Ultimately, I maintain that businesses secure the most tangible rewards from international cooperation, as these agreements directly facilitate daily economic transactions and drive corporate profitability worldwide.

**Analysis**

- *Disagreed view (Body 1):* the environment. Transboundary crises need global action. The example is the **Paris Climate Agreement**.
- *Agreed view (Body 2):* businesses. Fewer trade barriers and standardised regulation. The example is the **USMCA** and **General Motors**.
- The conclusion briefly acknowledges the first view ("although… indispensable role…") and then restates the opinion with *Ultimately, I maintain that…*

## Model essay 2

**Question:** *Some people believe that in a city, the best way to travel is by car, while other people argue that bicycles are a better way of travelling in a city. Discuss both views and give your opinion.*

> It is often argued that private vehicles offer the most efficient means of urban transportation, whereas others contend that bicycles represent a superior alternative for city travel. While automobiles provide unmatched personal comfort, I believe that bicycles are the optimal mode of transport within urban environments due to their efficiency in traffic and environmental sustainability.
>
> On the one hand, many people maintain that commuting by car is the most advantageous method of navigating a city. Proponents of this view emphasize the convenience, safety, and physical comfort that personal vehicles provide, especially during inclement weather or when traveling long distances. Furthermore, cars allow passengers to transport heavy goods or travel with family members seamlessly. A clear example of this can be seen in sprawling modern cities like Dubai or Houston, where expansive highway systems are designed specifically for automotive travel, making cars practically indispensable for daily commuting and commercial activities.
>
> On the other hand, I agree with the perspective that bicycles are a far better alternative for urban transit. In densely populated metropolitan areas, cycling offers a highly efficient solution to chronic traffic congestion, allowing commuters to bypass gridlock and eliminate time wasted searching for parking. Additionally, utilizing bicycles promotes public health through cardiovascular exercise and significantly reduces urban air pollution. For instance, the municipal government of Amsterdam heavily invested in dedicated cycling infrastructure, resulting in a system where bicycles now outnumber cars, drastically reducing inner-city travel times and local carbon emissions.
>
> In conclusion, although private cars offer superior comfort and utility for long-distance travel, they frequently contribute to urban congestion and environmental degradation. Ultimately, I maintain that bicycles are the superior choice for city travel because they facilitate faster transit through congested areas while fostering healthier and more sustainable urban communities.

**Analysis**

- The same frame again: *It is often argued that… whereas others contend that… While…, I believe that…* You can reuse it for any discussion question.
- Body 1 example: sprawling cities like **Dubai or Houston**. Body 2 example: **Amsterdam's** cycling infrastructure.

## Notes and common mistakes

- **Don't forget your opinion.** A discussion essay with no clear opinion loses marks in Task Response. Put it in the introduction **and** the conclusion.
- Body 1 must present the other view **fairly**: explain *why* people believe it. Don't attack it in the same paragraph.
- Keep the two paragraphs **balanced** in length (about 5–6 sentences each).
- Don't confuse this type with the [advantages/disadvantages](writing-task2-advantages-disadvantages.md) essay. A discussion essay is about two *opinions held by people*, not about benefits and drawbacks of one thing.

## Practice questions (real exams shared by the teacher)

- Some people believe that it is possible for a country to be economically successful and have a clean environment. Others argue that these two objectives are not compatible. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
- Some people argue that it is useful to do research into family history, to search for past generations. However, others believe that it is better to think more about the present and future generations. Discuss both views and give your opinion.
