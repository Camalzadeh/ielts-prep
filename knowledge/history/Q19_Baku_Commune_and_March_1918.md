# Q19. After October 1917: the Baku Commune and the massacres of 1918

> The political situation in Azerbaijan after October upheaval (1917). Bolshevik attempts to consolidate power in Baku. Anti-Azerbaijan policy of the Baku Soviet Commune headed by S. Shaumyan. The massacre policy in Baku and other provinces (1918): causes and consequences.

A note on figures: casualty numbers for 1918 are disputed. In this file every figure says who gives it. The figures for the provinces come from the ADR Extraordinary Investigation Commission as Azerbaijani historians usually cite them. Kazemzadeh (1951) is the main Western reading of the course.

---

## 1. Short answer

### English

After the Bolshevik seizure of power in Petrograd (25 October / 7 November 1917), Transcaucasia refused to recognize Lenin's government. In Tiflis the **Transcaucasian Commissariat** was formed (15/28 November 1917), and in the **Constituent Assembly** elections Musavat won the large majority of Muslim votes, while the Bolsheviks got only about 4% in the region. **Baku** was the exception: there the Bolshevik-led Soviet under **Stepan Shaumyan**, Lenin's Extraordinary Commissar for the Caucasus, wanted to keep the city and its oil for Soviet Russia. The Soviet's main rival was Musavat. To crush it, the Soviet relied on the armed units of the Armenian **Dashnaktsutyun**. Using the **"Evelina" incident** as a pretext, the Soviet and Dashnak forces attacked the Muslim quarters on **30 March - 2 April 1918**. About **12,000 Azerbaijanis** were killed in Baku (Azerbaijani estimate; Shaumyan himself admitted "more than 3,000" dead). The killings spread to **Shamakhi, Guba, Lankaran, Salyan, Karabakh, Zangezur** and other districts. The **Baku Council of People's Commissars** ("Baku Commune", 25 April - 31 July 1918) nationalized oil and attacked Ganja, but it was defeated at **Goychay** by the **Caucasian Islamic Army**. Baku was liberated on **15 September 1918**. Consequence: the Azerbaijani movement turned from autonomy to **independence** (28 May 1918). Since 1998, **31 March** is the **Day of Genocide of Azerbaijanis**.

## 2. Medium answer (exam essay)

### English

**Introduction.** The October upheaval of **1917** split Transcaucasia. Tiflis and most of the region refused to accept Bolshevik rule, but in **Baku**, the oil capital of the empire, the Bolsheviks under **Stepan Shaumyan** tried to hold power for Soviet Russia. **Thesis:** because the Bolsheviks were a minority among the Muslim majority of the province, they could keep Baku only by allying with the armed Armenian **Dashnaktsutyun**; this alliance turned a political struggle against **Musavat** into the **massacre of Azerbaijanis in March 1918** and in the provinces, and it pushed the Azerbaijani national movement from autonomy to **independence**.

**1. The political situation after October 1917.** On **15 (28) November 1917** the Georgian Mensheviks, Musavat and the Dashnaks formed the **Transcaucasian Commissariat** in Tiflis, chaired by **Evgeni Gegechkori**. It had 11 members (3 Georgians, 3 Azerbaijanis, 3 Armenians, 2 Russians); the Azerbaijanis were **Mammad Yusif Jafarov** (industry and trade), **Khudadat bey Melik-Aslanov** (transport) and **Khalil bey Khasmammadov** (state control). It did not recognize the Soviet government. In the **Constituent Assembly elections (November 1917)** Musavat received about **615,000 votes** in Transcaucasia, almost as many as the Georgian Mensheviks, while the Bolsheviks got under **100,000 (about 4%)**. When Lenin dispersed the Assembly (**January 1918**), the Transcaucasian deputies formed the **Seym** (opened **23 February 1918**), where Musavat had **30 seats**. In the Muslim regions, the new authority was the Musavat-led National Council; in January 1918, near **Shamkhor**, Muslim units disarmed Russian soldiers returning from the front, and several hundred were killed.

**2. Bolshevik attempts to consolidate power in Baku.** In Baku, the Soviet recognized the Petrograd government. In the October 1917 elections to the Baku Soviet, **Musavat won about 40% of some 25,000 votes** and the Bolsheviks only about 4,000, but Musavat did not join the Soviet, and after the moderate socialists walked out, the Bolsheviks and Left SRs controlled it. Lenin appointed **Shaumyan Extraordinary Commissar for the Caucasus (December 1917)**. The Soviet created a **Red Guard**, but its own forces were weak; the strongest armed force in the city was the Dashnak one, armed with weapons bought from deserting Russian soldiers.

**3. The March events (30 March - 2 April 1918).** In March the staff of the Muslim "**Savage Division**" arrived in Baku. On **30 March** the Soviet disarmed the Muslim crew of the ship **"Evelina"**, claiming they planned a revolt (Kazemzadeh says the report "lacked foundation"). Muslims demanded the weapons back; shooting began. On **31 March** the Soviet issued an **ultimatum**: recognize Soviet power, withdraw the Savage Division, reopen the railways. Musavat accepted it, but the Armenian National Council, which had declared itself neutral, joined the Soviet. Red Guards, Dashnak units and the Caspian fleet, which shelled the Muslim quarters, crushed the resistance and killed civilians. About **12,000 Azerbaijanis** died (Azerbaijani delegation at Paris, 1919); Shaumyan reported "more than three thousand" killed and called the results "brilliant". The **Ismailiyya** building and the offices of the newspaper **Kaspi** were burned, the **Taza Pir** mosque was damaged, and tens of thousands of Muslims fled the city.

**4. The Baku Commune and the provinces.** On **25 April 1918** the **Baku Council of People's Commissars** was formed (Shaumyan, Japaridze, Korganov, Narimanov and others). It disbanded the City Duma, banned opposition papers and in **June 1918 nationalized the oil industry**. Its troops, largely Dashnak in composition, carried the killings to the districts: **Shamakhi** (about 58 villages destroyed by the forces of S. Lalayev), **Guba** (the detachment of **Amazasp**), **Lankaran, Salyan, Kurdamir**, while **Andranik** acted in **Zangezur, Karabakh and Nakhchivan**. The ADR **Extraordinary Investigation Commission** (decree of **15 July 1918**, chair **Alakbar bey Khasmammadov**) documented these crimes.

**5. The fall of the Commune.** In **June 1918** the Commune's army marched on **Ganja**, the temporary capital of the ADR. It was defeated at **Goychay (27 June - 1 July 1918)** by the **Caucasian Islamic Army** of **Nuri Pasha**. The Commune resigned on **31 July**; the **Centrocaspian Dictatorship** (1 August) invited **Dunsterville's** British force (about 1,000 men). On **15 September 1918** the Islamic Army liberated Baku; Armenian sources speak of about **9,000** Armenians killed in the reprisals that followed.

**Conclusion.** The March events showed that the Bolshevik "class war" in Baku was in practice an ethnic war waged with Dashnak forces against the Azerbaijani majority. They destroyed faith in autonomy inside Russia, led to the **Declaration of Independence on 28 May 1918**, to the alliance with the Ottomans and to the liberation of Baku, which became the capital on **17 September 1918**. Since **1998, 31 March** is commemorated as the **Day of Genocide of Azerbaijanis**.

## 3. Detailed answer

### English

#### 3.1 The echo of October in Transcaucasia

The Bolshevik coup in Petrograd took place on **25 October (7 November) 1917**. Transcaucasia reacted in two opposite ways.

- **Tiflis** (Georgian Mensheviks, Dashnaks, Musavat, SRs) refused to recognize the Council of People's Commissars. On 15 (28) November 1917 a meeting of the Regional Centre of Soviets, the Army Soviet, the Tiflis Duma, the parties and the Muslim organizations created a provisional regional government "until the Constituent Assembly meets": the **Transcaucasian Commissariat**.
- **Baku**, the only large city of the region with a strong Russian and Armenian industrial working class, recognized the Soviet government. Its Soviet, chaired by Shaumyan, declared that it "accepts as obligatory the decrees of the All-Russian Congress of Soviets" (quoted by Kazemzadeh).

The Soviet government also tried to win the Muslims with words: the **Declaration of the Rights of the Peoples of Russia** (2/15 November 1917) and the appeal **"To all the toiling Muslims of Russia and the East"** (published early December 1917), which promised self-determination. Kazemzadeh remarks that Transcaucasia "could have proclaimed their independence" on this basis but did not, because its ties with Russia were still too strong.

**Table 1. Composition of the Transcaucasian Commissariat (15/28 November 1917)** (Kazemzadeh, p. 57)

| Post | Commissar | Nationality / party |
|---|---|---|
| President, External Affairs, Labour | Evgeni Gegechkori | Georgian, Menshevik |
| Interior | Akaki Chkhenkeli | Georgian, Menshevik |
| War | D. Donskoi | Russian, SR |
| Education | Alexeiev-Meskhiev | Georgian |
| Industry and Commerce | **Mammad Yusif Jafarov** | Azerbaijani, Musavat |
| Transportation | **Khudadat bey Melik-Aslanov** | Azerbaijani |
| Agriculture | A. Neruchev | Russian, SR |
| Supplies | A. Ter-Gazarov | Armenian |
| Social Security | A. Ohanjanian | Armenian, Dashnak |
| State Control | **Khalil bey Khasmammadov** | Azerbaijani, Musavat |

Total: 11 members - 3 Georgians, 3 Azerbaijanis, 3 Armenians, 2 Russians (Kazemzadeh lists ten names; the eleventh Armenian post varies by source). Gegechkori later admitted the body was "weak and inefficient": it had no army of its own and depended on the national councils for troops.

#### 3.2 The Constituent Assembly elections and the Seym

Elections to the All-Russian Constituent Assembly took place in Transcaucasia in late November 1917. The figures most often cited (O. Radkey's count, reproduced in Azerbaijani textbooks) are:

**Table 2. Constituent Assembly elections in Transcaucasia (November 1917), approximate**

| Party | Votes | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Georgian Mensheviks | ~662,000 | ~30% |
| **Musavat** (with the non-party democratic bloc) | **~616,000** | **~27-28%** |
| Dashnaktsutyun | ~558,000 | ~25% |
| Muslim Socialist bloc | ~160,000 | ~7% |
| SRs | ~118,000 | ~5% |
| **Bolsheviks** | **~94,000** | **~4%** |
| Others (Ittihad, Kadets, Hummat etc.) | small | - |

Two conclusions follow: (1) Musavat was the undisputed representative of the Azerbaijani Turks; (2) the Bolsheviks had almost no mass base outside Baku and the army. Musavat got **10 of the Transcaucasian seats** in the Assembly. Lenin dispersed the Assembly on **6 (19) January 1918**; the Transcaucasian deputies then formed their own parliament, the **Seym**, by tripling each party's mandates. It opened in Tiflis on **23 February 1918**. Kazemzadeh gives: Mensheviks 30 seats, **Musavat 30**, Dashnaks 27, SRs 6, Hummat 3, Kadets 1. The whole **Muslim fraction had 44 deputies** (see Q20).

#### 3.3 Shamkhor (January 1918) and the collapse of the Caucasian Front

After the Bolsheviks signed the Erzincan armistice with the Ottomans (5/18 December 1917), the Russian Caucasian Army melted away. Tens of thousands of soldiers moved north by rail through Ganja and Baku, selling their weapons on the way. The Tiflis authorities feared that these trains, many of them pro-Bolshevik, would bring Soviet power with them, and the Muslims needed arms. The Military Council of Nationalities, on the order of the Georgian Menshevik **Noe Ramishvili**, decided to disarm them.

- **Shamkhor, January 1918 (usually dated 9-12 January)**: Russian echelons refused to give up their weapons near the station of Shamkhor and were attacked by Azerbaijani armed bands. **Kazemzadeh: "several hundred lives were lost"; thousands of soldiers were disarmed.** Soviet-era accounts give higher figures (often about 1,000-2,000 killed); these should be treated as propaganda-inflated.
- Result: the Muslims obtained thousands of rifles, machine guns and some artillery, and **Ganja** became the centre of the Azerbaijani national forces. The Bolsheviks used Shamkhor as proof of "Musavat counter-revolution".

Meanwhile the arms of the departing army also flowed to the Dashnaks. In Baku, the Armenian National Council formed regular national units from Armenian soldiers of the old army. Kazemzadeh writes that the Dashnak forces "were very well armed", had military experience from the Russian army and from guerrilla war in Turkey, and that the Soviet's own forces "were much smaller than either those of the Musavat or the Dashnaktsutiun". The Azerbaijanis had arms but "no comparable organization or experience": under the Tsar, Muslims had been exempt from conscription, so they had almost no trained officers. Their only regular unit was the Muslim cavalry ("**Savage Division**") officers and the small Azerbaijani corps being formed in Ganja.

#### 3.4 The Bolsheviks take Baku

**Table 3. Elections to the Baku Soviet, October 1917** (Kazemzadeh, pp. 49-50)

| Party | Votes (approx.) | Comment |
|---|---|---|
| **Musavat** | **almost 10,000 (~40%)** | won most districts with Muslim workers (Bibi-Heybat 2,467; Binagadi 1,520; Surakhani 1,200) |
| SRs | large share | Russian population (White City 1,618; Black City 1,365) |
| Dashnaktsutyun | several thousand | Armenian workers |
| Bolsheviks | **close to 4,000** | Russian unskilled workers, Iranian migrant labour |
| Mensheviks | under 1,000 | |
| **Total** | **~25,000** | |

Kazemzadeh concludes that "the national factor largely determined the way a person voted". Musavat did not take its seats. Rasulzade explained that the Soviet was not elected democratically and that the peasantry was not represented, so Musavat "refuses to surrender power to the said Soviet". When the Bolsheviks proclaimed "All power to the Soviets", most other parties walked out, leaving a rump Soviet of **84 Left SRs, 31 Bolsheviks, 62 Bolshevik sympathizers, 15 Left Dashnaks, 6 Menshevik-Internationalists and 14 independents**; its executive committee was entirely Bolshevik and Left SR, and **Shaumyan** was re-elected chairman. Kazemzadeh: "neither the Soviet, nor its Executive Committee, could claim to be a democratic institution".

Steps of consolidation:

1. **2 (15) November 1917**: the Baku Soviet declares itself the authority in Baku and recognizes Petrograd.
2. **December 1917**: on Stalin's report, the Sovnarkom appoints **Shaumyan Temporary Extraordinary Commissar for Caucasian Affairs**. Shaumyan asks Stalin for 10 million roubles for Red troops.
3. **Red Guard** created against SR protests; the Revolutionary Committee of Public Safety (18 November 1917) is pushed aside; the Baku City Duma (which Kazemzadeh says was led by **Fatali Khan Khoyski**) is isolated.
4. Winter 1917-1918: famine. The peasants hold back grain; telegrams from Shamakhi, Astara and Ujar report starving crowds.
5. **30 January 1918**: *Bakinsky Rabochiy* declares that Baku is divided into "two large enemy camps", Musavat and Dashnaktsutyun, and that "a clash is inevitable". Kazemzadeh: although both were attacked in print, "it was against the former [Musavat] that it was mainly directed. The Soviet could not have won against two such formidable opponents at the same time".

On the Azerbaijani side, a congress of Transcaucasian Muslims at the end of 1917 decided to convene a **constituent assembly of Azerbaijanis and Caucasian Mountaineers**, a clear move toward self-rule. Both sides understood that a fight was coming.

#### 3.5 The March events (30 March - 2 April 1918)

**Table 4. Chronology of the March events**

| Date (1918) | Event |
|---|---|
| early March | Lenin's telegram to Shaumyan praises his "firm and decisive policy" but advises "a most cautious diplomacy" |
| 9 March | Staff of the Azerbaijani Savage Division arrives in Baku; its commander **General Talyshinsky** is arrested by the Soviet, then released; protest meetings in the mosques |
| ~27-29 March | Soldiers of the Savage Division come to Baku for the funeral of **Mammad Taghiyev**, an officer and son of **H. Z. Taghiyev**; they are to leave for Lankaran on the ship **"Evelina"** (Azerbaijani accounts) |
| **30 March** | The Soviet, "informed" that the armed Muslim crew of the Evelina is waiting to revolt, disarms them. Muslims gather at a mosque and demand the rifles back. Japaridze promises to return them, but shooting begins; by 6 p.m. Baku is "a regular battle-field", with trenches and barricades |
| **31 March** | Soviet ultimatum: (1) recognize the Soviet as the only authority, (2) withdraw the Savage Division from the Baku area, (3) reopen the Baku-Tiflis and Baku-Petrovsk railways; deadline 3 p.m., 1 April. The Committee of Revolutionary Defence (Shaumyan, Japaridze, Korganov) calls on the population to fight the "counter-revolution headed by the Musavat". The Armenian National Council, so far neutral, joins the Soviet. Ships of the Caspian fleet shell the Muslim quarters. Mass killing begins |
| **1 April** | Musavat accepts the ultimatum, but the fighting and killing in the Muslim quarters continue |
| **2 April** | Fighting ends; Japaridze writes to Shaumyan that the Muslims can be kept quiet "by promising them autonomy" |

**Who fought.** Shaumyan's own report to the Russian Sovnarkom (quoted by Kazemzadeh from Sef, 1930) lists on the Soviet side "the Soviet Red Guard; the Red International Army ... the Red Fleet ... and **Armenian national units**", and on the other side "the Muslim Savage Division ... and bands of armed Muslims, led by the Musavat Party". He said that **20,000 men** took part in the fighting.

**Casualties and destruction.** Figures vary widely:

| Figure | Who gives it |

Kazemzadeh's verdict: "the Soviet provoked the 'civil war' in the hope of breaking the power of its most formidable rival, the Musavat. However, once the Soviet had called upon the Dashnaktsutiun to lend its assistance ... the 'civil war' degenerated into a massacre, the Armenians killing the Muslims irrespective of their political affiliations or social and economic position." Even Soviet authors admitted it: A. Popov wrote that the Soviet "had no other alternative but to seek the support of one national group in the struggle against the other". The Azerbaijani Bolshevik **Sultan Majid Efendiyev** wrote that the Dashnaks "massacred to a man, in the name of the Soviet, the population of entire blocks ... inhabited by the Muslim poor", and that Shaumyan and Japaridze became "prisoners of the Dashnaks".

Shaumyan himself wrote: "For us the results of the battle were brilliant. The destruction of the enemy was complete." (Kazemzadeh, p. 75.) In the same reporting he acknowledged that the participation of the Armenian units gave the fighting a national character, but presented the outcome as a victory over counter-revolution.

Material losses: the **Ismailiyya** building (the Muslim Charitable Society, built by Musa Naghiyev, 1913) was burned; the offices of the newspapers **Kaspi** and **Achig Soz** were destroyed; the **Taza Pir** mosque was hit by shells; whole Muslim blocks in the upper town were burned and looted. A large part of the Muslim population, **tens of thousands of people**, fled to the villages of Absheron, to Ganja and to Iran (exact figures are not reliable). After the March events the Muslims of Baku were "completely disarmed" (Kazemzadeh), and Musavat leaders moved to Ganja and Tiflis.

#### 3.6 The Baku Commune (25 April - 31 July 1918)

After March the Bolsheviks "decided to rule alone". The Armenian National Council had to merge part of its troops into the Red Army; the **City Duma** was closed; the Menshevik *Nashe Slovo* and the Armenian *Baku* newspapers were banned. On **25 April 1918** the **Baku Council of People's Commissars (Baku Sovnarkom)** was formed, known as the "Baku Commune".

**Table 7. Main members of the Baku Sovnarkom** (Kazemzadeh, pp. 76-77)

| Post | Name | Party |
|---|---|---|
| Chairman, Foreign Affairs | **Stepan Shaumyan** | Bolshevik |
| Interior | Prokofy (Alyosha) Japaridze | Bolshevik |
| War and Navy | Grigori Korganov | Bolshevik |
| Others (education, justice, social welfare, communications, urban economy) | Kolesnikov, **Nariman Narimanov**, A. Karinian, Markarian | Bolsheviks |
| Cheka | Ter-Gabrielian | Bolshevik |
| Agriculture | **Mir Hasan Vazirov** | Left SR |
| Finance | Kireyev | Left SR (later executed for theft) |
| Supply | Tsybulsky | Left SR |

The Sovnarkom claimed authority over the whole Baku province, with "a peasant population of about a million", and promised to spread Soviet power over all Transcaucasia.

Main policies:

- **Nationalization of the oil industry** (decree of the Baku Sovnarkom, **1 June 1918**, according to Soviet sources; Kazemzadeh calls it "the climax of the Soviet policies"). On 22 May 1918 Lenin had assigned **100 million roubles** to pay the Baku workers and buy oil for Russia; earlier the Soviet had forced Baku capitalists to pay a levy of **50 million roubles**.
- **Nationalization of the Caspian merchant fleet and the banks**; the Mensheviks and SRs opposed this, and some industrialists were jailed.
- **Land decree**: land of the khans and beys confiscated and given to peasants; village Soviets formed during the march on Ganja.
- **Repression**: Muslim national organizations and newspapers closed; Muslims disarmed.
- **Food crisis**: in May-July the population "was forced to feed on nuts, bread being distributed only to the army" (Kazemzadeh).

Note on terms: "26 Baku Commissars" means the group of Bolshevik and Left SR leaders shot in Transcaspia on **20 September 1918**; not all 26 were members of the Sovnarkom.

#### 3.7 The massacres in the provinces (spring-summer 1918)

From Baku, Soviet-Dashnak detachments moved into the districts. Kazemzadeh: "The March Events ... touched off a series of massacres all over Azerbaijan. The brutalities continued for weeks." He also notes that during the June offensive "most of the Baku troops, and practically all their officers, were Armenians of Dashnak leanings", and names "the notorious **Amazasp** ... to whom any Muslim was an enemy simply because he was a Muslim". Even the Armenian Bolshevik Suren Shaumyan described their "requisitions, contemptuous treatment, and frequent shootings ... dictated by national hatred toward the Muslims".

| District | Perpetrators (main) | Figures usually cited |
|---|---|---|

**The Extraordinary Investigation Commission.** Created by a decree of the ADR government on **15 July 1918** (in Ganja), chaired by the lawyer **Alakbar bey Khasmammadov**, with a multi-ethnic group of lawyers. Its task: to investigate the mass killings and robberies against the Muslim population since the beginning of the war and bring the guilty to justice. According to *Achievements of the ADR* (IRS, 2014) it worked until **November 1919**, prepared **36 volumes** and **3,500 pages** of investigation material, **95 photographs** used by the Azerbaijani delegation in Paris, **128 draft reports and resolutions**, and raised the question of prosecuting **194** participants of the pogroms. The cases were dropped by the parliament's **amnesty of 9 February 1920**, granted on the occasion of the de facto recognition of the ADR.

#### 3.8 The Caucasian Islamic Army and the fall of the Commune

After the Seym dissolved, the Azerbaijani National Council declared independence on **28 May 1918** in Tiflis and moved to **Ganja**. By the **Batum treaty of 4 June 1918** the Ottoman Empire promised military help. The **Caucasian Islamic Army** was formed under **Nuri Pasha** (Enver Pasha's half-brother), who reached Ganja in late May 1918. Its Ottoman core was the **5th Caucasian Division** (commander Mürsel Pasha), later reinforced by the **15th Division** for the attack on Baku; Azerbaijani volunteers and the new Azerbaijani corps (formed 26 June 1918) fought alongside.

| Baku Commune "Caucasian Red Army" | about 10,000-18,000 at the start of the June offensive | Soviet and later estimates; mostly Armenian in composition |
| Bicherakhov's Cossack detachment | about 1,000-1,500 | joined the Commune in June, left the front in late July |

**The September events.** After the city fell, Azerbaijani irregulars took revenge on Armenians; Kazemzadeh says the Ottomans stayed outside the city for three days. A commission of the Armenian National Council counted **5,248** Baku Armenians plus **1,500** Armenian refugees and **2,240** unidentified bodies: **8,988** in total. The Armenian envoy Jamalian spoke of 25,000-30,000, which Kazemzadeh calls "grossly exaggerated". Jafarov answered that the killings were a spontaneous revenge for the March massacre and that a number of guilty men had been tried and hanged. The ADR government formally condemned the reprisals. For a balanced essay, mention this briefly.

#### 3.9 Causes (summary)

1. **Oil and strategy**: Soviet Russia needed Baku's fuel (Lenin's 100 million roubles; Stalin's instructions to treat Azerbaijan as an "internal affair of Russia").
2. **Political weakness of the Bolsheviks**: about 4% of the vote in Transcaucasia, about 4,000 votes in Baku; they could rule only by force.
3. **The Musavat factor**: the strongest party in Baku (~40%) and the leader of a movement toward autonomy; the Soviet saw it as the main "counter-revolution".
4. **Dashnak aims**: the Armenian national units were the decisive armed force and used the Soviet's fight against Musavat for their own national goals.
5. **Military imbalance**: the Muslims had no trained army (no conscription under the Tsar); the Soviet and the Dashnaks had soldiers, the fleet and artillery.
6. **Pretexts**: Shamkhor, the Savage Division, the Evelina incident.

#### 3.10 Consequences

- **Human and material losses**: about 12,000 killed in Baku alone (Azerbaijani estimate), tens of thousands more in the provinces according to the Commission's figures, destroyed villages, refugees, ruined economy.
- **Political turn**: Azerbaijani leaders lost faith in autonomy inside a democratic Russia; the Muslim fraction in the Seym pushed for separation (Trabzon, Batum), leading to the **Declaration of Independence (28 May 1918)**.
- **Orientation to the Ottoman Empire**: the Batum treaty and the Caucasian Islamic Army.
- **State-building**: creation of the national army, the Extraordinary Investigation Commission, and the move of the capital to Baku (**17 September 1918**).
- **Collapse of Bolshevik authority in Baku**: by summer 1918 even Baku workers turned against the Commune (the factory committees called the commissars "traitors and enemies of the people", 131 votes to 17).
- **Memory**: the topic was silenced in Soviet times, when the 26 Commissars were celebrated as heroes. By the decree of President Heydar Aliyev of **26 March 1998** "On the genocide of Azerbaijanis", **31 March** became the **Day of Genocide of Azerbaijanis**.

#### 3.11 Historiography

| School | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| **Soviet** | A "civil war" of the Baku proletariat against the Musavat "counter-revolution"; the 26 Commissars as martyrs; national violence blamed on "Dashnak excesses" or on both sides. Some Soviet authors (Popov, Efendiyev, Karinian) admitted the national character of the fighting |
| **Azerbaijani** (post-1991) | A planned genocide carried out by the Bolshevik-Dashnak alliance to destroy Azerbaijani statehood and seize Baku; based on the ADR Commission's materials; 31 March as a day of mourning |
| **Armenian** | The Armenians defended themselves or stayed neutral (Archbishop Bagrat); March presented as a Soviet-Musavat conflict; emphasis on the September 1918 killings of Armenians |
| **Western** (Kazemzadeh 1951, Swietochowski 1985, Suny 1972) | Kazemzadeh: the Soviet provoked the conflict to break Musavat, and it "degenerated into a massacre" by Armenian units; Swietochowski: the March Days were the decisive turning point toward Azerbaijani independence; Suny analyses the class and national dimensions of the Baku Commune |

#### 3.12 How to use this in the exam

- Start with the **thesis**: the Bolsheviks, weak in votes, held Baku only with Dashnak arms; this turned a political struggle into an ethnic massacre and pushed Azerbaijan to independence.
- Learn **10 key dates**: 25 Oct 1917; 15/28 Nov 1917 (Commissariat); Jan 1918 (Shamkhor); 23 Feb 1918 (Seym); 30 Mar - 2 Apr 1918; 25 Apr 1918 (Sovnarkom); 1 Jun 1918 (oil nationalization); 27 Jun - 1 Jul 1918 (Goychay); 31 Jul / 1 Aug 1918; 15 Sep 1918.
- Learn **5 key numbers** with their sources: ~40% Musavat in Baku Soviet elections; ~4% Bolsheviks in Transcaucasia; ~12,000 killed (Azerbaijani delegation, Paris 1919) vs "more than 3,000" (Shaumyan); Commission: 36 volumes, 3,500 pages; Dunsterforce ~1,000.
- Name **people**: Shaumyan, Japaridze, Korganov, Amazasp, Lalayev, Andranik; on the Azerbaijani side Rasulzade, Khoyski, Alakbar bey Khasmammadov, Nuri Pasha.
- Always attribute disputed figures ("according to...").
- Quote Kazemzadeh once ("degenerated into a massacre"): a Western scholar supporting the course's view is strong evidence.
- End with consequences and memory (28 May 1918, 15 September 1918, 31 March since 1998).

#### 3.3 Şəmkir (yanvar 1918) və Qafqaz cəbhəsinin dağılması

Bolşeviklər Osmanlı ilə Ərzincan barışığını imzaladıqdan sonra (5/18 dekabr 1917) rus Qafqaz ordusu dağıldı. On minlərlə əsgər dəmir yolu ilə Gəncə və Bakıdan keçərək şimala gedir, yolda silahlarını satırdı. Tiflis hakimiyyəti qorxurdu ki, əksəriyyəti bolşevikyönlü olan bu eşelonlar Sovet hakimiyyətini də özləri ilə gətirəcək, müsəlmanların isə silaha ehtiyacı var idi. Millətlərin Hərbi Şurası gürcü menşeviki **Noe Ramişvilinin** əmri ilə onları tərksilah etmək qərarına gəldi.

- **Şəmkir, yanvar 1918 (adətən 9-12 yanvar tarixi göstərilir)**: rus eşelonları Şəmkir stansiyası yaxınlığında silahlarını təhvil verməkdən imtina etdi və Azərbaycan silahlı dəstələrinin hücumuna məruz qaldı. **Kazımzadə: "bir neçə yüz nəfər həlak oldu"; minlərlə əsgər tərksilah edildi.** Sovet dövrü əsərləri daha yüksək rəqəmlər verir (çox vaxt 1000-2000 ölü); bu rəqəmlərə təbliğat məqsədilə şişirdilmiş kimi yanaşmaq lazımdır.
- Nəticə: müsəlmanlar minlərlə tüfəng, pulemyot və bir qədər artilleriya əldə etdilər, **Gəncə** Azərbaycan milli qüvvələrinin mərkəzinə çevrildi. Bolşeviklər Şəmkir hadisəsini "Müsavat əksinqilabının" sübutu kimi istifadə etdilər.

Eyni zamanda geri çəkilən ordunun silahları daşnaklara da axırdı. Bakıda Erməni Milli Şurası köhnə ordunun erməni əsgərlərindən nizami milli hissələr yaratdı. Kazımzadə yazır ki, daşnak qüvvələri "çox yaxşı silahlanmışdı", rus ordusunda və Türkiyədəki partizan müharibəsində hərbi təcrübə qazanmışdı, Sovetin öz qüvvələri isə "həm Müsavatın, həm də Daşnaksütyunun qüvvələrindən xeyli az idi". Azərbaycanlıların silahı var idi, lakin "müqayisə oluna bilən təşkilatı və təcrübəsi yox idi": çar dövründə müsəlmanlar hərbi xidmətdən azad olduqları üçün onların demək olar ki, təlim keçmiş zabitləri yox idi. Onların yeganə nizami qüvvəsi müsəlman süvari diviziyasının ("**Vəhşi diviziya**") zabitləri və Gəncədə yaradılmaqda olan kiçik Azərbaycan korpusu idi.

Rəsulzadə izah edirdi ki, Sovet demokratik yolla seçilməyib və kəndlilər orada təmsil olunmayıb, buna görə Müsavat "hakimiyyəti bu Sovetə təhvil verməkdən imtina edir". Bolşeviklər "Bütün hakimiyyət Sovetlərə!" şüarını irəli sürdükdə digər partiyaların çoxu Sovetdən çıxdı və Sovetin qalığında **84 sol eser, 31 bolşevik, 62 bolşevik tərəfdarı, 15 sol daşnak, 6 menşevik-internasionalist və 14 bitərəf** qaldı; icraiyyə komitəsi tamamilə bolşevik və sol eserlərdən ibarət idi, **Şaumyan** yenidən sədr seçildi. Kazımzadə: "nə Sovet, nə də onun İcraiyyə Komitəsi demokratik qurum olduğunu iddia edə bilməzdi".

Möhkəmlənmə addımları:

1. **2 (15) noyabr 1917**: Bakı Soveti özünü Bakıda hakimiyyət elan edir və Petroqradı tanıyır.
2. **Dekabr 1917**: Stalinin məruzəsi əsasında Xalq Komissarları Soveti **Şaumyanı Qafqaz işləri üzrə müvəqqəti fövqəladə komissar** təyin edir. Şaumyan Stalindən qırmızı qoşunlar üçün 10 milyon rubl istəyir.
3. Eserlərin etirazına baxmayaraq **Qırmızı Qvardiya** yaradılır; İctimai Təhlükəsizlik İnqilab Komitəsi (18 noyabr 1917) kənara itələnir; Bakı Şəhər Duması (Kazımzadəyə görə ona **Fətəli xan Xoyski** başçılıq edirdi) təcrid olunur.
4. 1917-1918-ci illərin qışı: aclıq. Kəndlilər taxılı satmır; Şamaxı, Astara və Ucardan gələn teleqramlarda ac kütlələr haqqında xəbər verilir.
5. **30 yanvar 1918**: "Bakinski Raboçi" qəzeti yazır ki, Bakı iki "böyük düşmən düşərgəsinə", Müsavat və Daşnaksütyuna bölünüb və "toqquşma qaçılmazdır". Kazımzadə: hər ikisi mətbuatda hücuma məruz qalsa da, "əsas zərbə birinciyə [Müsavata] yönəlmişdi. Sovet eyni zamanda belə iki güclü rəqibə qalib gələ bilməzdi".

Azərbaycan tərəfində 1917-ci ilin sonunda Zaqafqaziya müsəlmanlarının qurultayı **azərbaycanlıların və Qafqaz dağlılarının müəssislər məclisini** çağırmaq qərarına gəldi; bu, özünüidarəyə doğru açıq addım idi. Hər iki tərəf toqquşmanın yaxınlaşdığını başa düşürdü.

Bunu hətta sovet müəllifləri də etiraf edirdi: A. Popov yazırdı ki, Sovetin "bir milli qrupa qarşı mübarizədə digərinin dəstəyini axtarmaqdan başqa çarəsi yox idi". Müsəlman əhalinin böyük hissəsi, **on minlərlə insan**, Abşeron kəndlərinə, Gəncəyə və İrana qaçdı (dəqiq rəqəmlər etibarlı deyil). Mart hadisələrindən sonra Bakı müsəlmanları "tamamilə tərksilah edildi" (Kazımzadə), Müsavat liderləri Gəncəyə və Tiflisə köçdülər.

#### 3.6 Bakı Kommunası (25 aprel - 31 iyul 1918)

Martdan sonra bolşeviklər "təkbaşına idarə etmək qərarına gəldilər". Erməni Milli Şurası qoşunlarının bir hissəsini Qırmızı Orduya birləşdirməli oldu; **Şəhər Duması** bağlandı; menşeviklərin "Naşe Slovo" və ermənilərin "Bakı" qəzetləri qadağan edildi. **25 aprel 1918-ci ildə** "Bakı Kommunası" kimi tanınan **Bakı Xalq Komissarları Soveti** yaradıldı.

Əsas siyasəti:

- **Neft sənayesinin milliləşdirilməsi** (sovet mənbələrinə görə Bakı XKS-in **1 iyun 1918-ci il** tarixli dekreti; Kazımzadə onu "Sovet siyasətinin zirvəsi" adlandırır). 22 may 1918-ci ildə Lenin Bakı fəhlələrinə maaş vermək və Rusiya üçün neft almaq məqsədilə **100 milyon rubl** ayırmışdı; daha əvvəl Sovet Bakı kapitalistlərini **50 milyon rubl** vergi ödəməyə məcbur etmişdi.
- **Xəzər ticarət donanmasının və bankların milliləşdirilməsi**; menşeviklər və eserlər buna qarşı çıxdı, bəzi sənayeçilər həbs edildi.
- **Torpaq dekreti**: xan və bəylərin torpaqları müsadirə edilərək kəndlilərə verildi; Gəncəyə yürüş zamanı kənd sovetləri yaradıldı.
- **Repressiya**: müsəlman milli təşkilatları və qəzetləri bağlandı; müsəlmanlar tərksilah edildi.
- **Ərzaq böhranı**: may-iyul aylarında əhali "qoz-fındıqla qidalanmağa məcbur idi, çörək yalnız orduya paylanırdı" (Kazımzadə).

Termin haqqında qeyd: "26 Bakı komissarı" 1918-ci il **20 sentyabrda** Zakaspidə güllələnən bolşevik və sol eser rəhbərləri qrupudur; 26 nəfərin hamısı Xalq Komissarları Sovetinin üzvü deyildi.

Hətta erməni bolşeviki Suren Şaumyan onların "rekvizisiyalarını, təhqiramiz rəftarını və ... müsəlmanlara qarşı milli nifrətdən irəli gələn tez-tez güllələmələrini" təsvir edirdi.

**Fövqəladə İstintaq Komissiyası.** ADR hökumətinin **15 iyul 1918-ci il** tarixli qərarı ilə (Gəncədə) yaradılıb, sədri hüquqşünas **Ələkbər bəy Xasməmmədov** idi, tərkibində müxtəlif millətlərdən olan hüquqşünaslar var idi. Bu işlər ADR-in de-fakto tanınması münasibətilə parlamentin **9 fevral 1920-ci il amnistiyası** ilə xitam edildi.

#### 3.8 Qafqaz İslam Ordusu və Kommunanın süqutu

Seym dağıldıqdan sonra Azərbaycan Milli Şurası **28 may 1918-ci ildə** Tiflisdə müstəqillik elan etdi və **Gəncəyə** köçdü. **4 iyun 1918-ci il Batum müqaviləsi** ilə Osmanlı dövləti hərbi yardım vəd etdi. 1918-ci ilin may ayının sonunda Gəncəyə gələn **Nuru Paşanın** (Ənvər Paşanın ögey qardaşı) komandanlığı ilə **Qafqaz İslam Ordusu** yaradıldı. Onun Osmanlı nüvəsi **5-ci Qafqaz diviziyası** (komandiri Mürsəl Paşa) idi, sonra Bakı hücumu üçün **15-ci diviziya** ilə gücləndirildi; onlarla birlikdə Azərbaycan könüllüləri və yeni Azərbaycan korpusu (26 iyun 1918-ci ildə yaradılıb) vuruşurdu.

**Sentyabr hadisələri.** Şəhər alındıqdan sonra Azərbaycan nizamsız dəstələri ermənilərdən qisas aldı; Kazımzadə yazır ki, osmanlılar üç gün şəhərdən kənarda qaldılar. Erməni Milli Şurasının komissiyası **5248** bakılı erməni, üstəlik **1500** erməni qaçqın və **2240** kimliyi müəyyən edilməmiş meyit saydı: cəmi **8988**. Erməni nümayəndəsi Camalyan 25-30 min rəqəmini səsləndirirdi, Kazımzadə bunu "kobud şəkildə şişirdilmiş" adlandırır. Cəfərov cavab verdi ki, qətllər mart qırğınına görə kortəbii qisas idi və bir sıra günahkar mühakimə olunub edam edilib. ADR hökuməti qisas aktlarını rəsmən pislədi. Balanslı esse üçün bunu qısaca qeyd edin.

#### 3.9 Səbəblər (xülasə)

1. **Neft və strategiya**: Sovet Rusiyasının Bakı yanacağına ehtiyacı var idi (Leninin 100 milyon rublu; Stalinin Azərbaycanı "Rusiyanın daxili işi" saymaq göstərişi).
2. **Bolşeviklərin siyasi zəifliyi**: Cənubi Qafqazda təxminən 4% səs, Bakıda təxminən 4000 səs; onlar yalnız güc vasitəsilə idarə edə bilərdilər.
3. **Müsavat amili**: Bakıda ən güclü partiya (~40%) və muxtariyyət hərəkatının lideri; Sovet onu əsas "əksinqilab" sayırdı.
4. **Daşnak məqsədləri**: erməni milli hissələri həlledici silahlı qüvvə idi və Sovetin Müsavata qarşı mübarizəsindən öz milli məqsədləri üçün istifadə etdi.
5. **Hərbi qeyri-bərabərlik**: müsəlmanların təlim keçmiş ordusu yox idi (çar dövründə hərbi mükəlləfiyyət yox idi); Sovet və daşnakların isə əsgərləri, donanması və artilleriyası var idi.
6. **Bəhanələr**: Şəmkir, Vəhşi diviziya, "Evelina" hadisəsi.

#### 3.10 Nəticələr

- **İnsan və maddi itkilər**: təkcə Bakıda təxminən 12 min ölü (Azərbaycan hesablaması), Komissiyanın rəqəmlərinə görə qəzalarda daha on minlərlə ölü, dağıdılmış kəndlər, qaçqınlar, dağılmış iqtisadiyyat.
- **Siyasi dönüş**: Azərbaycan liderləri demokratik Rusiya daxilində muxtariyyətə inamı itirdilər; Seymdəki müsəlman fraksiyası ayrılmağa təkan verdi (Trabzon, Batum) və bu, **Müstəqillik Bəyannaməsinə (28 may 1918)** gətirib çıxardı.
- **Osmanlı dövlətinə yönəlmə**: Batum müqaviləsi və Qafqaz İslam Ordusu.
- **Dövlət quruculuğu**: milli ordunun, Fövqəladə İstintaq Komissiyasının yaradılması, paytaxtın Bakıya köçürülməsi (**17 sentyabr 1918**).
- **Bakıda bolşevik hakimiyyətinin çöküşü**: 1918-ci ilin yayında hətta Bakı fəhlələri Kommunaya qarşı çıxdı (fabrik komitələri 131 səslə 17 səsə qarşı komissarları "xain və xalq düşməni" adlandırdı).
- **Yaddaş**: sovet dövründə 26 komissar qəhrəman kimi təqdim olunduğu üçün bu mövzu susdurulurdu. Prezident Heydər Əliyevin **26 mart 1998-ci il** tarixli "Azərbaycanlıların soyqırımı haqqında" fərmanı ilə **31 mart** **Azərbaycanlıların Soyqırımı Günü** elan edildi.

#### 3.12 İmtahanda bundan necə istifadə etməli

- **Tezislə** başlayın: səs baxımından zəif olan bolşeviklər Bakını yalnız daşnak silahı ilə saxlaya bildilər; bu, siyasi mübarizəni etnik qırğına çevirdi və Azərbaycanı müstəqilliyə yönəltdi.
- **10 əsas tarixi** öyrənin: 25 oktyabr 1917; 15/28 noyabr 1917 (Komissarlıq); yanvar 1918 (Şəmkir); 23 fevral 1918 (Seym); 30 mart - 2 aprel 1918; 25 aprel 1918 (XKS); 1 iyun 1918 (neftin milliləşdirilməsi); 27 iyun - 1 iyul 1918 (Göyçay); 31 iyul / 1 avqust 1918; 15 sentyabr 1918.
- Mənbələri ilə birlikdə **5 əsas rəqəmi** öyrənin: Bakı Soveti seçkilərində Müsavat ~40%; Cənubi Qafqazda bolşeviklər ~4%; ~12 000 ölü (Azərbaycan nümayəndə heyəti, Paris 1919) və "3 mindən çox" (Şaumyan); Komissiya: 36 cild, 3500 səhifə; Denster qüvvəsi ~1000.
- **Şəxsləri** adlandırın: Şaumyan, Caparidze, Korqanov, Amazasp, Lalayev, Andranik; Azərbaycan tərəfindən Rəsulzadə, Xoyski, Ələkbər bəy Xasməmmədov, Nuru Paşa.
- Mübahisəli rəqəmlərin mənbəyini həmişə göstərin ("... görə").
- Kazımzadədən bir dəfə sitat gətirin ("qırğına çevrildi"): kursun mövqeyini dəstəkləyən Qərb alimi güclü sübutdur.
- Nəticələr və yaddaşla bitirin (28 may 1918, 15 sentyabr 1918, 1998-ci ildən 31 mart).
